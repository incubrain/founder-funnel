## The Legacy Projection vs The AI Reality

Every analyst house has a model for Indian fintech. The standard projection: $145B market today growing to $550B by 2030 at a 30% CAGR. That number will be wrong — almost certainly in the conservative direction.

Here's why legacy projections systematically underestimate what's coming:

**They treat India as a version of the US in 2010.** They look at credit penetration rates, smartphone adoption curves, and GDP per capita, then apply a standard digital finance adoption model. This is structurally wrong. India did not inherit legacy banking infrastructure — it built a digital-native financial stack from scratch, at national scale, through government mandate. That stack is categorically different from what any other country has.

**They don't price in DPI leverage.** India built three pieces of infrastructure that no other country has simultaneously: Aadhaar (1.4 billion biometric IDs), UPI (the world's largest real-time payment system by transaction volume), and the Account Aggregator framework (2.2 billion financial accounts enabled for consent-based data sharing). These are not individual fintech products — they are developer APIs for the entire financial system. AI on top of this infrastructure is not the same equation as AI on top of legacy banking rails.

**They ignore the compounding.** UPI processed 228 billion transactions in 2025 — up 29% year-over-year. That is 640 million transactions per day, more than Visa's global daily volume. Each transaction is a data point. Each data point improves the AI models that underwrite credit, detect fraud, and personalize financial products. The system is self-reinforcing in a way that linear growth models cannot capture.

**They miss the 10x economy thesis.** India's GDP is tracking toward $7 trillion by 2030 on consensus estimates. The optimistic case — a $10 trillion economy — would require approximately 8-9% real growth compounded over five years. India's digital economy is growing twice as fast as its overall economy. UPI's international expansion (20 countries by FY29) extends this DPI leverage to the Indian diaspora and eventually to partner countries. A $10 trillion economy with 97% digital payment penetration and an AI-native financial stack is not a $550B fintech market — it is something considerably larger.

The specific unlock: India's $530 billion MSME credit gap. Sixty-three million small businesses contribute 30% of GDP and employ 110 million people. They are systematically underserved by formal credit because traditional underwriting cannot assess them. The Account Aggregator framework lets those businesses share their GST data, bank statements, and transaction history to prove creditworthiness without collateral. AI-powered credit models trained on this data can underwrite in minutes what used to take weeks of document collection and manual review. This is not an incremental improvement — it is a different product category.

## What's Actually Happening (2025–2026)

### 1. Agentic Payments Are Live

In October 2025, Razorpay, NPCI, and OpenAI launched a pilot for agentic payments in India — users discovering products, selecting them, and completing UPI transactions inside a ChatGPT conversation, never leaving the chat interface. Axis Bank and Airtel Payments Bank are pilot banking partners. BigBasket is among the first merchants.

The underlying mechanism is UPI Reserve Pay, which transforms UPI from a transaction tool into an execution layer: authorise once, set a financial limit, allow trusted AI agents to execute multiple debits within those limits. In February 2026, Gnani.ai and Razorpay extended this to in-call voice payments — an AI agent completing a collection call and processing the UPI payment in the same conversation.

PhonePe signed a partnership with OpenAI in November 2025 to embed ChatGPT across its consumer and merchant apps. The progression is clear: payment execution is becoming a background capability that AI orchestrates, not a separate user action. The conversational commerce wave that was hypothetical in the US is live infrastructure in India.

### 2. The Account Aggregator Flywheel Has Started

The Account Aggregator framework, launched in September 2021, crossed a critical mass threshold in 2025. The ecosystem now includes 170+ active Financial Information Providers, 600+ Financial Information Users, and 2.2 billion enabled financial accounts. In the first half of FY25, 5.47 million loans were facilitated through AA data flows — more than the total for the previous two years combined. Loan disbursements reached Rs 462 billion in H1 FY25.

The number that matters: MSME and motor loan AA penetration is still at 1.14% and 1.50% respectively. The flywheel has started spinning but has not yet reached velocity. The infrastructure is in place. The models are being built. Analysts estimate this could add Rs 5-7 lakh crore in incremental MSME credit by FY28.

This is what digital public infrastructure compounding looks like: a consent framework that started as a regulatory initiative is becoming the primary rails for a multi-trillion-rupee credit expansion.

### 3. AI-Native Credit Products Are Replacing Document-Based Underwriting

The shift happening across India's lending stack is not "banks adding AI to existing processes." It is new products built from scratch on real-time data streams that didn't exist five years ago. GST returns, bank statement analysis, UPI transaction patterns, e-commerce seller data — these are the inputs. The models run in seconds. The decision is instant.

Perfios launched CAM AI in August 2025, an AI-powered credit underwriting platform that automates the full credit assessment memo process for banks and NBFCs. In December 2025, it announced an AI-powered real-time operating system for BFSI — an integrated suite of agentic platforms that function as a unified intelligence layer across the lending lifecycle, from onboarding to collections. In October 2025, Perfios signed an MOU with NeSL to integrate debt resolution and insolvency data into credit risk models.

Signzy is running AI-powered digital onboarding for banks and NBFCs — identity verification, document processing, and KYC automation at scale. The RBI's Digital Lending Directions, published in 2025, explicitly reinforce the AA and consent-based data framework as the correct architecture for this.

RBI surveys in 2025 show that only 21% of regulated entities currently deploy AI solutions. That number will not stay at 21%.

### 4. Compliance Has Become a Product Category

India's regulatory layer accelerated significantly in 2025. The RBI released its FREE-AI framework in August 2025 — seven sutras and 26 recommendations covering infrastructure, governance, consumer protection, and assurance for AI in financial services. MeitY published India's AI Governance Guidelines in November 2025. The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified in November 2025, operationalizing the DPDP Act 2023 with full compliance required by May 2027.

For fintech companies, DPDP layered on top of existing RBI, SEBI, and IRDAI compliance creates a compliance surface that is simultaneously an existential requirement and a product opportunity. Penalties reach Rs 250 crore for data breach failures. The Consent Manager framework introduces a new architectural layer for consent orchestration. Financial institutions need tooling, automation, and audit infrastructure — and that tooling does not yet exist at the quality and scale the market requires.

The FACE Code of Conduct for RegTech companies, published in June 2025, creates a credibility framework for startups building compliance infrastructure. India now has a defined sandbox for compliant compliance products.

### 5. Embedded Finance Is Eating Every Vertical

India's embedded finance market hit $24 billion in 2025, growing at 12.4% annually, projected to reach $45 billion by 2030. The mechanism: BaaS infrastructure providers (Setu, Decentro, M2P Fintech) make it possible for any company with distribution to offer financial products — lending, insurance, payments, accounts — without becoming a bank.

Swiggy embeds food delivery credit. Amazon Pay offers instant checkout financing. Ola and Uber build driver insurance and income-smoothing products. The pattern is universal: the financial product follows the customer relationship, not the other way around. Every company with a large user base and transactional data is a potential fintech.

The infrastructure layer (Setu, Decentro, M2P) enables this without every company needing to build bank integrations themselves. These are the picks-and-shovels of the embedded finance gold rush.

### 6. UPI Is Becoming Global Infrastructure

UPI is now accepted in seven countries with bilateral agreements — UAE, Singapore, Bhutan, Nepal, Maldives, Mauritius, France. NPCI is targeting twenty countries by FY29. Cross-border transaction volumes jumped over 20x to 755,000 transactions in FY25, with the trajectory accelerating in early FY26.

India receives over $120 billion in remittances annually. UPI's cross-border rails reduce the cost of these transfers to under 1% with instant settlement, compared to SWIFT-based wire transfer costs of 3-5% with 2-3 day delays. This is not a marginal improvement — it eliminates the entire value proposition of the remittance incumbent industry.

UPI became the first payment system integrated on the PayPal World platform for international transactions. The international UPI play is India exporting its DPI stack — and it is working.

### 7. Crypto Regulation Is Settling Into a Workable Framework

India's crypto stance clarified in 2025. SEBI began monitoring crypto tokens resembling securities from April 2025. A multi-regulator framework (RBI, SEBI, Finance Ministry) is being formalized. Crypto exchanges, custodial wallets, and intermediaries are classified as reporting entities under PMLA. The 30% flat tax on digital asset income and 1% TDS on transfers remain in place.

The net result: crypto is legal, taxed, and increasingly regulated. The 30% tax is punitive for traders but manageable for long-term holders and institutional participants. The PMLA inclusion creates compliance requirements that eliminate the worst actors and normalize the industry. India is not heading toward a crypto ban — it is heading toward a regulated digital asset market that operates within the financial system.

## The Companies Shaping the Future

### The DPI Stack

**PhonePe**
PhonePe processes the majority of UPI transactions by volume — it is functionally part of India's financial infrastructure. The OpenAI partnership in November 2025 embeds ChatGPT across the platform, positioning PhonePe not just as a payments app but as an AI-native financial services interface. A planned IPO targets a $15 billion valuation. What PhonePe represents: UPI scale becoming the foundation for a full financial services layer — insurance, lending, investments, wealth management — all accessed through a payment interface that 500 million Indians already use daily.

**Razorpay**
Valued at $9.2 billion. Revenue base from payments processing; growth bet on agentic commerce. The NPCI-OpenAI agentic payments pilot positions Razorpay as the infrastructure layer for conversational commerce in India — the Stripe equivalent for a world where AI assistants execute purchases rather than humans filling checkout forms. The Razorpay Model Context Protocol (MCP) server is a telling infrastructure investment: it is building the API layer for AI agents to interact with India's payment rails.

**Pine Labs**
Pine Labs IPO'd in 2025 and acquired Setu in 2022 for $70-75 million, making it both a point-of-sale infrastructure company and an Account Aggregator API provider. Pine Labs processes payments for 140,000+ merchants. Setu's AA APIs handle 5 million daily Account Aggregator requests and 10 million monthly bill payment transactions on AWS. The Setu acquisition turned Pine Labs into a full-stack fintech infrastructure company — combining merchant payment terminals with digital banking APIs.

**CRED**
CRED built a credit card bill payment platform into one of India's most trusted financial brands among high-credit-score urban consumers. The strategic value is the user base: high-income, creditworthy, data-rich. CRED has leveraged this into lending products (CRED Cash), travel, and commerce — all anchored by a trust relationship built on helping users manage their existing credit. In a market full of companies trying to acquire creditworthy customers, CRED started with them.

**Jupiter**
Jupiter is the clean-room version of what a digital bank should be. Instant account opening via Aadhaar and PAN, zero minimum balance, real-time spend insights, goal-based savings pots, credit card, salary accounts — all built around Federal Bank and Axis Bank partnerships for the actual banking license. What Jupiter proves: Indian consumers will choose better UX over a legacy bank branch, given the choice.

### AI-Native Finance

**Perfios**
Perfios is the infrastructure layer that most people in fintech credit don't see but everyone depends on. Over 1,000 financial institutions globally use Perfios for bank statement analysis, income verification, financial data extraction, and underwriting automation. The CAM AI platform (August 2025) and the BFSI operating system (December 2025) represent the evolution from a data processing company to an AI-native financial intelligence platform. The NeSL partnership in October 2025 brings insolvency and debt resolution data into the credit intelligence stack. Perfios is where financial data becomes lending decisions, at machine speed, at scale.

**Signzy**
Signzy runs AI-powered digital onboarding infrastructure for banks and NBFCs — the unsexy but essential layer of identity verification, document processing, and KYC automation that every regulated financial company needs to do before it can do anything else. The shift from manual document review to Signzy's AI stack is measured in hours-to-seconds and cost-reduction in the thousands of rupees per onboarded customer. This is the infrastructure work that makes everything else possible.

### The Credit Revolution

**MoneyView**
MoneyView serves the middle-market consumer credit segment — salaried employees who don't have the perfect credit history that banks require but are demonstrably creditworthy by behavioral signals. The product is clean: instant personal loans, fully digital KYC, NBFC-backed lending. The market is the 200-400 million Indians who are formally employed but credit-underserved by traditional scoring.

**KreditBee**
KreditBee targets salaried employees and young professionals with small-ticket personal loans that increase in size as the borrower builds a repayment track record. The business model is credit ladder construction: start with small disbursements to prove the model, graduate borrowers to larger products as they demonstrate behavior. RBI-certified NBFC, fully digital operations, and a flywheel of behavioral data that improves underwriting with every loan.

**Lendingkart**
Lendingkart is the MSME lending play — business loans and working capital for small manufacturers, traders, and service businesses that cannot get bank credit due to documentation requirements and collateral gaps. Fully online application, minimal documentation, zero collateral requirements, agreements with 25+ regulated entities including HDFC Bank and Tata Capital. The integration with Account Aggregator data flows is what enables sub-day underwriting decisions on borrowers that traditional banks spend weeks assessing.

**Slice (now Axis Bank)**
Slice's journey is instructive: started as a digital credit card challenger targeting college students and young professionals with a neo-credit product, crossed 10 million customers, then merged into Axis Bank. The merger represents the consolidation thesis playing out — the large banks are acquiring the fintech distribution and product capabilities rather than building them. Slice's 12-18 month underwriting model (start with a small limit, observe behavior, expand access) is now inside one of India's largest private banks.

### India's Pune Connection

**OneCard / FPL Technologies (Pune)**
OneCard is the most sophisticated credit card product built by an Indian fintech. App-controlled metal credit card — instant virtual issuance, 5x rewards on top spending category, no joining or annual fees, granular in-app controls over limits and transactions, issued in partnership with six banking partners (CSB Bank, Federal Bank, Indian Bank, BOB Financial, SBM Bank, South Indian Bank). Unicorn at $1.4 billion valuation. FPL Technologies has also built OneScore (credit score monitoring) and Wizely (savings products) as adjacent products on the same consumer financial relationship.

What OneCard proves from Pune: you don't need a banking license to build a better credit card product than the banks. You need the right API relationships, superior UX design, and a genuine understanding of what creditworthy consumers actually want from their credit experience.

**Fibe / EarlySalary (Pune)**
Fibe (formerly EarlySalary, rebranded 2022) is one of India's original salary advance and consumer lending apps. Founded in 2015 by Akshay Mehrotra and Ashish Goyal in Pune, with Rs 1,270 crore annual revenue and $250M total funding over 12 rounds. The product evolution mirrors the market: started as employer-linked salary advances, expanded into personal loans, education financing, medical loans, and solar energy project financing. The Rs 225 crore debt raise in August 2025 continues the balance sheet expansion. Fibe is a Pune-native company that has been right about consumer credit trends ahead of the rest of the market for a decade.

**Swipez (Pune)**
Swipez builds billing, payment collection, and business automation tools for SMBs — automating invoice management, recurring payments, and payment reminders. This is embedded finance for the long tail of small businesses: not the sophisticated digital-first companies that Razorpay serves, but the millions of small businesses that are still managing cash flows manually.

### Infrastructure

**Setu (now Pine Labs)**
Setu was India's first Account Aggregator gateway — the API layer that translated the regulatory framework into usable developer infrastructure. Before the Pine Labs acquisition, Setu had built pipelines across payments (BBPS), data (AA), and identity verification. Under Pine Labs, the technology handles 5 million daily AA requests and powers the AA layer for a significant portion of India's consent-based lending infrastructure. If you have taken a digital loan in India in the last two years, there is a reasonable probability Setu's infrastructure was involved somewhere in the underwriting data flow.

**Decentro**
Decentro is Banking-as-a-Service infrastructure for India — APIs covering account opening and management, KYC verification, payment collection and disbursement, and credit underwriting for companies that want to offer financial products without building direct bank integrations. The target: the wave of non-fintech companies (logistics, e-commerce, HR platforms) that want to embed financial services into their existing products. Decentro powers the financial product layer so they don't have to build it.

**M2P Fintech**
M2P is the card and payments infrastructure layer — enabling companies to issue prepaid cards, debit cards, and credit cards through banking partnerships without the regulatory complexity of being a bank. The card programs for dozens of neobanks, corporate expense platforms, and consumer fintech products in India run on M2P's stack. Infrastructure that is invisible to end users but essential to every card-issuing product built in India.

### Global Leaders Setting the Reference Architecture

**Stripe**
Stripe processed $1.4 trillion in 2024 at 38% year-over-year growth while reaching profitability. Valued at approximately $160 billion. What Stripe proves at scale: financial infrastructure should be developer-first, API-first, and globally composable. Stripe Atlas, Stripe Radar, Stripe Connect, and the expanding treasury products are building toward a financial operating system for internet businesses. The architectural lesson for Indian founders: Razorpay is India's Stripe — but Stripe itself is the benchmark for what the product should eventually become.

**Plaid**
Plaid powers 8,000+ applications with connections to 10,000+ financial institutions. Revenue tracking toward $430M in 2025, with 20% of new customers now being AI companies. The strategic data point: 20% AI customers. As AI agents need to understand and act on financial data, Plaid becomes the authentication and data layer between AI systems and financial accounts. In India's context, the Account Aggregator framework is the Plaid equivalent — but built into public infrastructure rather than as a private company.

**Brex / Mercury**
Brex (AI-powered spend platform for startups and scaling companies) and Mercury (modern banking for startups, $3.5B valuation, $300M Series C in March 2025, 200,000+ customers) define what neobanking for the startup ecosystem looks like. Mercury processes $156 billion in annual transaction volume, up 64% year-over-year. The relevance to India: as Pune's startup ecosystem scales — which it is, across AI, manufacturing tech, biotech, and fintech itself — it will generate demand for the same infrastructure. The Indian equivalent is being built now.

## What This Means for Founders

### Solopreneur Opportunities

**DPDP Compliance SaaS for NBFCs and Fintechs**
The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 with an 18-month compliance runway. Every NBFC, payment aggregator, wealth management platform, and insurance distributor in India faces simultaneous RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, and DPDP compliance obligations — with penalties up to Rs 250 crore for data breach failures. The tooling for this does not exist at the quality and price point that small-to-mid-sized NBFCs can afford. A consent management platform, data audit trail tool, or automated compliance reporting product built specifically for India's regulatory stack is a defined problem with a defined deadline and a large addressable market that has to spend.

**Alternative Credit Scoring for Gig Workers and MSME Owners**
India has 15 million gig workers and 63 million MSMEs. Traditional credit scoring fails both because income is irregular and documentation is poor. But both have behavioral data — UPI transaction patterns, GST payment history, e-commerce seller ratings, utility payment records — that predicts creditworthiness far better than a CIBIL score based on credit history they haven't had access to. An AA-based credit scoring product built specifically for the informal economy worker profile is a genuine gap. The AA framework provides the data access. The question is who builds the model.

**AI Finance Coach for Blue-Collar and Gig Workers**
WhatsApp has 550 million users in India, the vast majority of whom have never interacted with a financial planning tool. A conversational AI finance coach — built for Hindi and regional languages, delivered over WhatsApp, helping gig workers and daily wage earners track income, plan for irregular expenses, and access appropriate financial products — addresses a genuine problem that no bank or fintech has solved for this segment. The business model: distribution for relevant financial products (insurance, micro-investment, emergency credit) that the coach recommends based on observed financial behavior.

**Embedded Finance for Manufacturing SMBs**
Pune is the center of India's automotive and manufacturing belt — Tata, Bajaj, Mahindra, and hundreds of their Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers operate here. These manufacturing SMBs need invoice financing, supply chain credit, equipment loans, and cash flow management tools calibrated to the manufacturing payment cycle (typically 30-90 days). A focused embedded finance product for manufacturing supply chain participants — using GST data and e-invoicing records as the underwriting input — is a specific gap at the intersection of Pune's manufacturing base and India's DPI infrastructure.

**RegTech for the AA Ecosystem**
The Account Aggregator framework is legally complex. Financial institutions need to maintain audit trails of consent records for seven years. They need to handle consent revocation in real time. They need to manage data flow within DEPA framework constraints. The companies building AA-powered products (lending, wealth management, insurance) all need the same compliance plumbing but are building it independently. A RegTech product that provides AA compliance infrastructure as a service — consent management, audit trails, data retention, regulatory reporting — serves every company in the growing AA ecosystem.

### The India/Pune Angle

Pune has 726 fintech startups, with 128 funded and two unicorns (OneCard, Bajaj Finserv adjacent entities). The ecosystem is less crowded than Bengaluru or Mumbai, operational costs are lower, and the talent base — particularly for engineering and product roles — is deep and well-priced relative to the quality.

The specific Pune structural advantage: the intersection of manufacturing finance and digital finance. Pune's MSME base consists of real companies with real cash flows — not hypothetical customers but businesses actively looking for better financial products and services. A fintech founder in Pune has direct access to the customers they're building for.

The DPI advantage compounds this. India's Account Aggregator framework, UPI infrastructure, and Aadhaar-based identity are not available in any other country at this scale. A fintech product built on India's DPI stack has a structural head start over anything built on US or European banking infrastructure, where the equivalent of AA is either nonexistent (US) or still being legislated (EU's Open Banking under PSD2).

## The Bottom Line

India has built the world's most advanced public financial infrastructure. UPI is larger than Visa by daily transaction count. The Account Aggregator framework has enabled 2.2 billion financial accounts for consent-based data sharing. Aadhaar provides biometric identity verification at national scale. These are not incremental improvements to existing financial infrastructure — they are a different technology category.

The AI layer on top of this stack is early. Only 21% of regulated financial entities are deploying AI solutions as of 2025. The $530 billion MSME credit gap is structurally addressable with tools that now exist but haven't yet been deployed at scale. The DPDP compliance deadline (May 2027) is creating an immediate market for RegTech infrastructure. Agentic payments are live but handling a fraction of a percent of total transaction volume.

The founders building on India's DPI stack are not competing with legacy banks. They are operating on infrastructure that legacy banks cannot yet match. That is a rare structural advantage in financial services — a sector where the moat of incumbents has historically been the infrastructure itself. In India in 2026, the infrastructure is public, open, and available to any developer who wants to build on it.

The question is not whether India's fintech market will be larger than the analyst projections. It will be. The question is which founders will have built the products by then.
