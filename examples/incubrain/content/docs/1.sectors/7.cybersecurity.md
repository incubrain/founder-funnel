## The Legacy Projection vs The AI Reality

The standard market research pitch for AI in cybersecurity: $25.35 billion in 2024, growing to somewhere between $93 billion and $134 billion by 2030 at a tidy 24–27% CAGR. Solid numbers. Structurally incomplete framing.

Here is the problem with every legacy cybersecurity forecast: it models defense as a function of current threat volume. It does not model what happens when the offense acquires the same AI tooling as the defense — simultaneously, at scale, globally, for free.

**The threat and the defense are the same technology.** Every AI model that can generate personalized email copy can generate phishing at industrial scale. Every LLM that can write code can write polymorphic malware. Every voice synthesis model that can clone a celebrity can clone your CFO. The attack surface does not grow linearly with AI advancement — it grows with it. Faster models, cheaper inference, and easier access to frontier AI capabilities mean the attack surface is expanding at exactly the same pace as the technology powering the defense.

**Agentic AI changes the calculus entirely.** Legacy cyberattacks were human-paced. A phishing campaign required a human to write the lure, target the recipient, and manage responses. AI agents remove those constraints. An autonomous attack agent can probe a target's defenses, adapt to countermeasures, adjust the social engineering approach, and execute the breach — at machine speed, without human involvement. The SOC analyst who sleeps does not. The AI agent that attacks does not.

**The 10x economy thesis compounds this.** Elon Musk's projection of 10x economic growth driven by AI and robotics does not just create a larger cybersecurity market — it creates a world where every system worth attacking is dramatically more valuable and more deeply interconnected. More infrastructure controlled by software. More financial flows automated. More critical decisions made by AI agents that can themselves be compromised. The stakes of a successful attack scale with the economic leverage of the systems being attacked.

Grand View Research and Statista are probably low by 2x or more. Not because their methodology is wrong, but because they are extrapolating the current threat environment into the future. The correct frame is: cybersecurity spending will grow proportionally to the economic value that AI is creating and protecting. If AI creates tens of trillions in new economic value by 2030, the amount enterprises and governments will pay to secure that value will not be capped at $134 billion.

This is not a market. It is an arms race with asymmetric stakes — and the defenders are behind.

## What's Actually Happening (2025–2026)

### 1. Agentic AI Has Replaced SOC Automation

The first wave of AI in cybersecurity was automation: take repetitive Tier-1 alert triage, wrap it in a playbook, reduce analyst toil. Useful, but fundamentally limited. Playbooks require someone to write every scenario. Reality writes scenarios that no one anticipated.

The second wave — agentic AI — is architecturally different. CrowdStrike's Charlotte AI AgentWorks and SentinelOne's Purple AI Athena release are not chatbots that answer questions about security incidents. They are autonomous agents that investigate incidents the way a senior analyst would: forming a hypothesis, querying identity logs, correlating endpoint telemetry, cross-referencing threat intelligence, determining whether remediation is needed, and executing containment — all within seconds, with a full chain of reasoning logged for review.

CrowdStrike's Torq-powered Socrates platform claims resolution of 95% of Tier-1 alerts without human involvement. IBM's Autonomous Threat Operations Machine (ATOM) does autonomous triage, investigation, and remediation. Gartner forecasts that AI will increase SOC efficiency by 40% compared to 2024 by 2026.

The SOC is not being automated. It is being rebuilt around AI agents that act as autonomous analysts, with humans supervising rather than executing.

Why this matters: the problem the SOC has been trying to solve is not volume — it is cognitive load. An average enterprise SOC receives 2,992 security alerts daily. Sixty-three percent go unaddressed. Eighty percent of analysts report feeling consistently behind. Forty-six percent of all alerts are false positives. The math does not work with humans. With agents, it can.

### 2. AI-Generated Attacks Are the New Normal

Eighty percent of phishing attacks in 2025 are AI-generated or use AI tooling for some part of the attack chain. This is not a marginal trend — it is a structural shift that happened in under three years.

The numbers are stark:
- **1,265% surge** in AI-linked phishing attacks since 2023
- **76.4% of phishing campaigns** use polymorphic tactics — each message is generated uniquely to evade signature-based detection
- **Deepfake incidents** jumped to 179 cases in Q1 2025 alone, surpassing all of 2024, representing a 2,137% increase since 2022
- A Hong Kong finance firm lost **$25.6 million** in a single deepfake video conference where every participant was AI-generated
- Voice cloning can replicate executive voices from as little as **3 seconds of audio**

The defense implication: any security product that works by recognizing what a bad thing looks like is in structural decline. Polymorphic malware generates a new variant every 15 seconds during an attack. A signature database cannot keep up. Behavioral AI — modeling what normal looks like and flagging deviations — is the only architecture that works against attacks that are designed to look different every time.

### 3. CERT-In 2025 Mandates Have Redefined India's Compliance Landscape

In July 2025, India's Computer Emergency Response Team released its Comprehensive Cyber Security Audit Policy Guidelines (Version 1.0) — the most sweeping cybersecurity compliance mandate in the country's history.

What it requires:
- **Annual third-party cybersecurity audits** for all Indian organizations with digital infrastructure — private and public, MSME to enterprise
- **6-hour incident reporting deadline** for all identified breaches and cybersecurity incidents
- Audit reports submitted to CERT-In within **5 days of completion**
- Risk-based, domain-specific approach — not checkbox compliance

Penalties: up to one year in prison and significant fines under the Information Technology Act for non-compliance.

As of mid-2025, 9,700+ audits have already been conducted. Over 200 firms are authorized to provide them. 93% of Indian executives anticipated a cybersecurity budget increase for 2025, according to PwC India — a number that predated the July 2025 mandates. The compliance demand curve is vertical.

The business implication is direct: every Indian company with digital operations now has a legal obligation to audit annually. The supply of qualified auditors is nowhere near the demand. The tools to run those audits efficiently — automated vulnerability scanning, continuous compliance monitoring, audit report generation — are a direct business opportunity.

### 4. LLMs and AI Agents Are a New Attack Surface

The same AI infrastructure enterprises are deploying to build internal tools, customer-facing agents, and productivity assistants is itself vulnerable. Prompt injection has emerged as the number one vulnerability in OWASP's 2025 Top 10 for LLM Applications, appearing in over 73% of production AI deployments during security audits.

The attacks that emerged at scale in Q4 2025:
- **System prompt extraction** — revealing the operational logic, tool descriptions, and policy boundaries that enterprises have embedded in their AI agents
- **Jailbreaking agents** — overriding safety constraints to make agents execute actions their operators never intended
- **Tool misuse** — convincing an agent to use its legitimate tool access (file systems, APIs, databases) for malicious purposes
- **Memory poisoning** — injecting false context into an agent's persistent memory to alter its future behavior
- **Supply chain attacks on model weights** — compromising the AI models themselves rather than the applications

The problem is structural. An AI agent with access to email, calendar, internal documents, and API endpoints is a high-value target. Unlike a traditional application vulnerability that requires technical exploitation, prompt injection can be executed in natural language by anyone who can interact with the agent.

### 5. Cyber Risk Quantification Has Become a Board-Level Requirement

Security teams used to report in technical terms — number of incidents, mean time to detect, patch coverage percentages. Boards cannot make capital allocation decisions with those metrics.

The new vocabulary is financial: what is the expected loss from a ransomware event in dollar terms? What is the ROI of increasing the security budget by $2 million? What is the breach probability for each of our critical systems?

Cyber Risk Quantification (CRQ) is now a formal analyst category. Forrester named Safe Security the category leader in CRQ in its most recent Wave. Safe Security's November 2025 acquisition of Balbix — a Gartner Magic Quadrant Visionary in Continuous Threat Exposure Management — created the largest dedicated CRQ platform on the market. Axio holds the second position in the Forrester rankings.

This matters because: when the board can see that the probability of a $50 million breach event drops from 12% to 4% with a $3 million security investment, security stops being a cost center and starts being a capital allocation decision. The shift from technical reporting to financial reporting is permanently changing how security budgets get set.

### 6. Zero Trust Has Become the Default Enterprise Security Architecture

Zero trust used to be a forward-looking security philosophy. In 2025, it is the default architecture for any enterprise building new infrastructure.

The numbers: 63% of global organizations have implemented zero trust either partially or fully (Gartner). Eighty-one percent plan to adopt it by 2026. Sixty-five percent are replacing VPN services within the year, a 23% increase from the prior year. The ZTNA market is estimated at $7.34 billion in 2025 alone.

The driver is not ideology — it is the math of modern hybrid work. A perimeter-based security model assumes users are inside a trusted network. When every user is remote, every device is personally owned, and every application is SaaS, there is no perimeter to defend. Zero trust — verify every user, every device, every request, regardless of where it originates — is the only architecture that matches the actual topology of modern enterprise computing.

The India angle is particularly acute: 150+ Global Capability Centers operating in India represent a distributed workforce accessing sensitive global systems from Indian offices. CERT-In mandates, combined with the GCC operational reality, are making zero trust not a strategic option but a near-term compliance requirement.

### 7. The Cloud Native Attack Surface Is the New Perimeter

The average enterprise in 2025 has workloads across three or more cloud providers, hundreds of SaaS applications, and a container and Kubernetes infrastructure that changes configuration continuously. Traditional security tools were designed for static infrastructure. Cloud native environments are dynamic by design.

Wiz built a $32 billion business solving this problem. Their approach — agentless, graph-based analysis of cloud infrastructure to find toxic combinations of risk (a misconfigured database accessible from the internet AND holding PII AND running as an over-privileged service account) — proved that the cloud native attack surface had no adequate defender before them. Alphabet's acquisition of Wiz, announced March 2025 at $32 billion, is the largest cybersecurity acquisition in history. It is also the clearest signal of where the defensive imperative sits.

## The Companies Shaping the Future

### AI-Native Defense

**CrowdStrike**
ARR: $5.25 billion as of January 2026. Revenue: $4.81 billion in fiscal 2026, up 22% year-over-year. Target: $20 billion ARR by FY2036.

CrowdStrike's Falcon platform runs on a single lightweight agent across 29 modules, meaning enterprises can buy additional security capabilities without deploying new infrastructure. Charlotte AI is the generative AI layer: Charlotte AgentWorks lets security teams create and deploy custom AI agents in plain language, no coding required. Threat AI is the first autonomous threat intelligence system — an array of agents that reason across global threat data, hunt for adversary infrastructure, and produce finished intelligence at machine speed.

What CrowdStrike has proven: platformization works. Customers buying 5+ modules grew 20% year-over-year. The more capabilities deployed on one platform, the lower the total cost and the higher the detection fidelity — because the agent sees everything. Their goal of $20 billion ARR by FY2036 implies roughly 4x growth from current levels, in a market growing 25% per year.

**SentinelOne**
ARR: $1.055 billion as of October 2025. Purple AI — the agentic security analyst — reached 40% attach rate on new licenses in late 2025.

Purple AI Athena is the most significant product milestone: it integrates with the Singularity Hyperautomation layer to execute pre-approved workflows autonomously — contacting defenders via Slack, executing containment, generating detection rules — not just surfacing recommendations. The "agentic detection and response" capability works across any SIEM or data source, not just SentinelOne infrastructure. This is important: it means SentinelOne is positioning Purple AI as the security analyst layer that runs on top of any existing security stack, not a reason to replace your SIEM.

**Darktrace**
Revenue: $780M+ annually, targeting $1 billion by 2027. Taken private by Thoma Bravo in 2024 for $5.3 billion.

Darktrace's core thesis — train an AI to model what "normal" looks like for a specific organization, then flag deviations — was prescient when they launched in 2013. It is now table stakes. The company is investing $200M+ in US operations in 2026, expanding from network detection to email, cloud, OT, and endpoint. Their value proposition: unsupervised AI does not need to be told what an attack looks like. It finds the anomaly that no signature ever anticipated.

**Abnormal Security**
ARR: $200 million as of 2024, with 100% year-over-year growth. $250 million Series D at $5.1 billion valuation, led by Wellington Management. Over 20% of the Fortune 500 are customers.

Abnormal does one thing with unusual precision: email security. Their AI builds a behavioral baseline for every user — what they typically write, who they contact, what time of day, what their requests look like — and flags messages that deviate from that profile. The target is business email compromise and AI-generated phishing, where the attacker's message is syntactically perfect and contains no malicious payload, just a request. Traditional security tools give these a clean bill of health. Abnormal catches them.

**Vectra AI**
$425 million raised. The company's thesis is network detection and response (NDR): watch the network, not the endpoint, to catch attackers moving laterally after initial compromise. Most breaches are not stopped at entry — they are stopped (or not) during the dwell period when attackers probe for crown jewels. Vectra's AI correlates network signals across cloud, SaaS, identity, and data center to find that lateral movement before the attacker reaches high-value targets.

### The New Attack Surface

**Wiz (being acquired by Alphabet for $32 billion)**
Wiz's approach to cloud security was architecturally new: instead of monitoring infrastructure in real-time, scan it continuously and build a graph of all the relationships between cloud resources, identities, network paths, and data. The insight is that individual misconfigurations are rarely critical alone — but specific combinations create catastrophic risk. A database that is publicly accessible AND contains PII AND can be accessed by an over-privileged Lambda function AND has no encryption at rest is a four-factor toxic combination that traditional tools would flag as four separate low-severity findings. Wiz surfaces it as one critical risk.

The $32 billion acquisition price is the answer to the question "how important is cloud native security?" It is the most valuable cybersecurity exit in history.

**Orca Security**
$640 million raised. Orca pioneered agentless cloud security — providing visibility into cloud infrastructure without installing agents on every workload. Orca's SideScanning technology reads cloud workload data directly from the API, avoiding the operational overhead that had limited adoption of traditional CSPM tools. Orca competes directly with Wiz for the CNAPP (Cloud Native Application Protection Platform) market.

**Snyk**
$1.32 billion raised. ARR: $343 million, up 12% year-over-year. Snyk's market is developer security: integrating vulnerability detection into the CI/CD pipeline so that insecure code, dependencies, and container configurations are caught before they ship. The developer-first approach is the correct one — fixing a vulnerability at code review is orders of magnitude cheaper than fixing it post-deployment. Snyk's challenge in 2025 is a decelerating growth rate (12% versus 26% in 2024) as the developer security market matures and CrowdStrike, SentinelOne, and others add similar capabilities to their platforms.

**Chainguard**
$892 million raised through 2025 (including a $356 million Series D in April and $280 million in October). $3.5 billion valuation. ARR: $40 million, growing 7x year-over-year.

Chainguard's insight: the open source software supply chain is the largest unaudited security attack surface in enterprise computing. Most organizations use thousands of open source packages. Many of those packages have not been patched in years, contain known vulnerabilities, and are running in production right now. Chainguard builds "hardened" versions of popular open source images — stripped of everything that is not needed, with every component tracked and signed. Their customer base includes Wiz, GitLab, Canva, and Snap. The SolarWinds and Log4j attacks demonstrated that supply chain compromise is the highest-leverage attack vector against enterprises that have invested heavily in perimeter defense. Chainguard is the only company that has made a serious commercial business out of solving it.

### Agentic Security: The New SOC Stack

**Torq**
Torq's HyperSOC is powered by Socrates — an "omni-agent" that functions as an autonomous AI SOC analyst. The event-driven architecture delivers up to 5x alert throughput over established SOAR platforms. Torq claims Socrates resolves 95% of Tier-1 alerts and many Tier-2 tasks without human involvement. The distinction between Torq and legacy SOAR platforms like Splunk SOAR or IBM Security SOAR is architectural: Torq does not require pre-built playbooks for every scenario. Socrates reasons through novel situations the way an analyst would — forming hypotheses, testing them, and acting.

**Tines**
Where Torq targets the large enterprise SOC, Tines targets the security team that has not yet built a SOAR practice. Their approach is workflow automation first, AI second: enable security teams to automate repetitive tasks without code, then layer AI agents on top of established automation for autonomous investigation. Tines integrates with every major security tool and has a library of pre-built automation workflows (Stories) that makes initial deployment fast. In a market with Torq, CrowdStrike Charlotte, and SentinelOne Purple AI all competing for the same SOC automation budget, Tines' position is the "simpler and faster to deploy" option for security teams without dedicated automation engineers.

**Swimlane**
Swimlane describes itself as the largest pure-play security automation company. Its differentiator is breadth: Swimlane covers not just security operations but compliance, risk management, and vulnerability management in a single platform — making it attractive for enterprises that need automation across the entire security function, not just the SOC. Gartner rates Swimlane as a Leader in SOAR. In the 2025–2026 transition from SOAR to agentic AI SOC platforms, Swimlane is the incumbent that must prove its automation heritage translates to the agentic era.

### India's Cybersecurity Moment

**FireCompass (Bengaluru)**
$30.5 million raised, including a $20 million investment from EC-Council in September 2025 as part of EC-Council's $100M Cybersecurity Innovation Fund. FireCompass is building the AI-powered offensive security platform for the enterprise: continuous automated red teaming, penetration testing, and attack surface management in a single SaaS product.

The insight is contrarian: instead of building better defensive detection, simulate the attacker continuously and find the paths to compromise before real attackers do. FireCompass combines External Attack Surface Management (discover what's exposed), Continuous Threat Exposure Management (prioritize by exploitability), and automated red teaming (test whether the exposure is actually exploitable) in a workflow that previously required a full red team engagement every six to twelve months. With CERT-In mandates requiring annual audits, FireCompass is positioned directly in the path of that compliance spend.

**CloudSEK (Bengaluru)**

The dark web monitoring use case is more important in India than most markets appreciate. India's digital payment infrastructure (UPI processes over 13 billion transactions per month) and the density of banking GCCs in Bangalore and Pune mean that leaked financial credentials are high-value targets. CloudSEK detects the signal before the attacker acts on it.

**Safe Security (Bengaluru)**
$70 million Series C. Forrester-ranked leader in Cyber Risk Quantification. The November 2025 acquisition of Balbix — itself a Gartner Magic Quadrant Visionary in Exposure Assessment Platforms — created the most complete AI-native cyber risk quantification platform on the market.

Safe Security's product translates technical security posture into financial risk terms: probability of breach, expected financial loss, ROI of specific security investments. As the CERT-In mandate pushes Indian organizations toward annual audits and boards demand financial-language risk reporting, Safe Security's platform sits at the intersection of both requirements.

**Protecto (Pune)**

With every Indian enterprise now deploying internal LLMs and CERT-In's data protection requirements expanding, Protecto sits at the intersection of AI deployment acceleration and compliance.

**TAC Security (Chandigarh)**
India's most internationally recognized vulnerability management company. Listed on Nasdaq. TAC's ESOF (Enterprise Security in One Framework) platform covers risk management, vulnerability management, and compliance in a single product. The company has worked with over 300 enterprises globally including Fortune 500 firms. TAC Security represents what India-built security product companies can achieve with global ambition from day one.

**InstaSafe (Bengaluru)**
India's leading Zero Trust Network Access (ZTNA) provider. Founded 2012. Revenue: Rs 16.1 crore (FY2025). InstaSafe's product grants users access to specific applications rather than the entire network, eliminating the VPN model that creates lateral movement opportunity for attackers who gain initial access. With 65% of enterprises globally replacing VPN services and CERT-In mandates driving compliance pressure, ZTNA adoption in India is accelerating. InstaSafe's positioning — affordable ZTNA built specifically for the Indian enterprise context — gives it structural advantages over global ZTNA vendors in the domestic market.

### LLM and AI Security: The Newest Category

**Lakera (acquired by Check Point, 2025)**
Lakera built the earliest production-grade guardrail for LLM applications. Lakera Guard provides real-time runtime protection for AI applications, detecting and blocking prompt injection attacks, data leakage, and jailbreak attempts as they happen. Lakera Red is the automated red teaming counterpart: systematic adversarial testing of AI applications during development. Check Point's acquisition signals that LLM security is now a mainstream enterprise requirement, not a research curiosity.

**HiddenLayer**
Real-time detection and prevention of adversarial attacks against AI models themselves. The attack surface HiddenLayer addresses is distinct from application security: it targets the ML models running in production — model extraction, model inversion, adversarial example attacks, model poisoning. As enterprises stake more business-critical decisions on AI models, compromising those models directly becomes a high-value attack vector. HiddenLayer is one of the only companies building security specifically for the model layer.

**Protect AI (acquisition interest from Palo Alto Networks at ~$700 million)**
MLSecOps platform: security scanning for ML pipelines, model repositories, and AI supply chains. Protect AI's Huntr bug bounty platform has identified vulnerabilities in popular ML frameworks including TensorFlow, PyTorch, and Hugging Face models. As AI model supply chains become as complex as software supply chains, the Chainguard problem recurs at the AI layer — and Protect AI is attempting to solve it.

**Robust Intelligence (acquired by Cisco for ~$400 million, 2025)**
AI application security — testing AI models and systems for reliability, safety, and adversarial robustness before and during production deployment. Cisco's acquisition signals the same dynamic as Check Point's Lakera deal: the major security platforms are buying AI security capabilities rather than building them, because the specialist companies have moved faster.

## What This Means for Founders

### The Compliance Tsunami Is a Product Wedge

CERT-In's July 2025 mandatory audit requirements apply to every Indian organization with digital infrastructure. 9,700 audits have been conducted in the first six months. The supply of CERT-In-empanelled auditors — only 200+ firms are authorized — cannot match demand from tens of thousands of eligible organizations.

The founder opportunity is not to become a CERT-In auditor. It is to build the tooling that makes CERT-In compliance faster, cheaper, and more defensible:

- **Automated CERT-In compliance SaaS**: Map an organization's technical posture to CERT-In's specific requirements, generate the audit-ready documentation, track remediation progress, and prepare the submission to CERT-In. The Indian market has nothing equivalent to what Drata and Vanta do for SOC 2 in the US market. The requirement is real, the deadline is now, and the tooling gap is wide open.

- **Incident response playbooks for Indian enterprises**: The 6-hour CERT-In reporting deadline is extremely aggressive. Most Indian enterprises have no documented incident response process that meets this timeline. A SaaS product that provides breach detection + automated CERT-In notification templates + response workflow orchestration would find ready buyers in every compliance officer in India.

### AI Phishing Simulation Is a Direct Market Gap

If 80% of phishing attacks are now AI-generated, training employees with manually written phishing simulations is a category error. Employees need to be tested against the actual attack format they will face: personalized, contextually accurate, polymorphic messages that reference their actual job title, their actual colleagues, and their actual work context.

An AI phishing simulation platform that:
1. Ingests an organization's LinkedIn data and internal org chart
2. Generates contextually accurate, AI-quality phishing lures targeting each employee's specific role
3. Tests employee response rates and provides role-specific training
4. Tracks improvement over time

This is a product any technical founder with access to a good LLM API can build. The market is every company with a security awareness training requirement — which, post-CERT-In, is every Indian company with digital infrastructure.

### LLM Security Scanning for Indian Enterprises

Every Indian enterprise deploying internal AI tools faces the same unsolved problem: their employees are feeding sensitive data into LLMs — vendor contracts into summarization tools, HR files into analytics dashboards, customer data into chatbots. Most have no controls.

A lightweight LLM security scanner that:
1. Monitors enterprise LLM API calls for data being sent
2. Classifies sensitive data categories (PII, financial data, health data) in real-time
3. Flags policy violations and generates audit logs for CERT-In compliance
4. Integrates with existing data loss prevention (DLP) policies

This is a real product, not a prototype. Protecto is building in this space. The market is large enough for multiple players, and the India-specific compliance angle (DPDP Act, CERT-In) creates a local advantage that global vendors have not yet moved to capture.

### The India/Pune Structural Advantages

Pune's position in India's cybersecurity market is underappreciated:

**GCC density creates enterprise sales access.** Pune has the second-highest concentration of GCCs in India after Bengaluru. These are the security operations centers, IT procurement teams, and CISOs of major global enterprises — with budgets set in USD, not INR, and procurement processes that favor vendors who understand their specific operational context. A Pune-based founder selling to a Pune-based GCC is not competing against Palo Alto Networks on equal terms. They are competing on local knowledge, relationship access, and speed of iteration based on customer proximity.

**1 million unfilled cybersecurity roles is both a problem and an opportunity.** India's cybersecurity workforce demand is expected to hit 1 million. Current supply: roughly 80,000 qualified professionals. That gap is not filled by hiring — it is filled by automation that lets a smaller team cover more ground. Every tool that amplifies the effectiveness of existing security staff (AI-assisted threat hunting, automated compliance documentation, intelligent alert triage) is solving a problem every Indian CISO is actively willing to pay for.

**93% of Indian executives planned cybersecurity budget increases for 2025 (PwC India).** That survey was conducted before the July 2025 CERT-In mandates expanded the compliance requirement to every organization in India. The budget curve was already inflecting up. The mandates accelerated it.

**Defense sector cybersecurity is a growing vertical in Pune.** Pune hosts a significant cluster of defense electronics and aerospace companies (Bharat Electronics Limited, DRDO labs, defense contractors). As India's defense procurement moves toward digital systems, supply chain security and OT/ICS cybersecurity for defense applications is a specific vertical that requires local presence, security clearance-compatible operations, and understanding of MoD procurement. No global vendor is well-positioned here. A Pune founder with the right relationships could build a significant business without ever competing internationally.

## The Bottom Line

The cybersecurity market is the only sector where the size of the problem is guaranteed to grow with every other technology advancement. Every AI model deployed is a new attack surface. Every agent given tool access is a potential pivot point for a compromised system. Every byte of sensitive data fed into an LLM is a potential breach. The more powerful the AI economy becomes, the more valuable it is to defend — and the more capable the attackers are of compromising it.

The defense is also AI-native. CrowdStrike crossed $5 billion ARR. SentinelOne crossed $1 billion. Wiz sold for $32 billion. The market is validating that AI-native security platforms built on behavioral models — not signatures, not playbooks, not perimeter defense — are the architecture that survives the next five years.

For founders: the compliance pressure in India is creating a decade of product opportunities for anyone who understands what CERT-In actually requires and can build tools faster than the large vendors move. The LLM security category is four years old and still has no clear category leader. The AI phishing simulation market does not yet exist at the quality level the threat requires. The gap between 80,000 qualified cybersecurity professionals and 1 million required roles is an automation product opportunity that spans every layer of the security stack.

The attackers have the same AI the defenders do. The difference is that the defenders are building the infrastructure to use it at scale — and the founders who ship that infrastructure in the next 18 months will be building on a market that cannot stop growing.
