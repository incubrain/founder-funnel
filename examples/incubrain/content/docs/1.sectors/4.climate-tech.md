## The Legacy Projection vs The AI Reality

The standard analyst pitch for climate tech goes like this: the global market sits at $37.5B today, growing to $220B by 2035, driven by policy mandates and corporate ESG pressure. Steady, predictable, respectable. Almost certainly wrong — in the conservative direction.

Here is why legacy models are structurally incapable of forecasting what is actually happening:

**They model policy, not physics.** The cost of solar panels has fallen 90% in the last decade. Lithium-ion battery packs crossed $108/kWh in late 2024 — and then kept falling, reaching $70/kWh for stationary storage by mid-2025, a 45% drop in a single year. These are exponential cost curves behaving exactly as Wright's Law predicts. Every doubling of cumulative production drives a predictable cost reduction. Policy is a tailwind. Physics is the engine.

**They treat energy and AI as separate markets.** They are not. Google DeepMind's WeatherNext 2 forecasts renewable generation with sub-hourly resolution, reducing grid curtailment and increasing the economic value of wind by 20%. AI-optimized grid dispatch is replacing the reserve margin buffer that made renewables expensive to integrate. When AI makes variable renewable generation dispatchable, the entire economics of the grid changes. Legacy models did not price this in.

**They miss the storage inflection.** Utility-scale battery storage dropped to $65/MWh by October 2025 in markets outside China and the US. Ember's analysis shows that "anytime electricity" from solar-plus-storage is now economically viable without subsidy in high-irradiation markets. When the marginal cost of storing solar electricity falls below the marginal cost of gas peakers, the transition does not slow down — it accelerates. We crossed that threshold in 2025.

**They undercount India's buildout velocity.** India added 44.51 GW of renewable capacity in 2025 — the highest ever in a single year — bringing solar alone to 132.85 GW. The national pipeline now totals 486.94 GW against a 500 GW target by 2030. The government is targeting 50 GW additions per year through the decade. India's renewable transition is not a promise in a policy document. It is steel in the ground and contracts in execution.

**They ignore the Musk trajectory.** Elon Musk and other exponential-technology analysts project the global economy will roughly 10x by 2035, driven by AI and robotics reaching human-level productivity across most domains. If that directional forecast is even half right, energy demand doubles or triples — and the only way to supply that demand on any timeline that avoids climate catastrophe is with zero-carbon generation that is already cost-competitive with fossil fuels. Climate tech does not compete with fossil fuels through regulation. It wins through cost.

The honest framing: this is not a $220B market by 2035. It is the complete re-architecture of how the world generates, stores, and uses energy — a transition measured in dozens of trillions of dollars and compressed by AI and manufacturing learning curves into a timeline that no analyst projected ten years ago.

## What's Actually Happening Right Now (2025–2026)

### 1. Solar Has Won the Cost War. Now It Needs to Win the Grid War.

LONGi launched its HIBC (Heterojunction Back Contact) module at SNEC 2025 — a 700W panel with 25.9% module efficiency, the first time silicon solar has crossed the 25% threshold in commercial production. In April 2025, the Institute for Solar Energy Research Hamelin certified a 27.81% conversion efficiency for the underlying cell technology, a new world record for crystalline silicon.

The average global solar generation cost hit $43/MWh in 2024. In high-irradiation markets with cheap financing, utility-scale solar is deploying at $25–30/MWh. This is cheaper than the operating cost of most existing coal plants, before considering the capital cost of new construction.

The unsolved problem is grid integration. Solar generates at noon. Demand peaks at 7 PM. The grid war — solving the temporal mismatch between generation and demand through storage, demand response, and AI dispatch — is the defining infrastructure challenge of the next decade. The companies that win it will be worth far more than the companies that simply built solar panels cheaply.

### 2. Battery Storage Just Crossed the Tipping Point

Three numbers define where battery storage stands in late 2025:

- **$70/kWh** — lithium-ion battery pack price for stationary storage, down 45% from 2024. This is the cheapest li-ion category for the first time, driven by CATL's manufacturing dominance (661 GWh of batteries sold in 2025) and BYD's competitive pressure.
- **$65/MWh** — utility-scale battery storage LCOE outside China and the US, as reported by Ember in December 2025. This is below the marginal cost of many gas peaker plants.
- **4 hours** — the duration threshold that, once crossed economically, converts solar from daytime electricity into round-the-clock generation. The market is there. The deployments are following.

Tesla deployed 12.5 GWh of energy storage in Q3 2025 alone — up 81% year-over-year. In September 2025, Tesla unveiled the Megapack 3 and Megablock system: 20 MWh installed in one week, produced in Houston. The utility-scale storage market is no longer a future bet. It is a present buildout.

### 3. AI Is Transforming Grid Operations

Google DeepMind's WeatherNext 2 model, released in late 2025, generates weather forecasts eight times faster than previous models and outperforms traditional supercomputer-based forecasts on 99.9% of variables — including the temperature, wind speed, and irradiance data that determine renewable generation output. Applied to grid operations, this means operators can dispatch storage and flexible demand with far more precision.

Open Climate Fix is using DeepMind's forecasting technology in grid optimization projects in both the UK and India. Google's wind power tool increased the value of wind generation by 20% purely through better short-term output forecasting — no hardware changes, no new capacity, just better information.

The grid optimization market is large, low-capital, and almost entirely unaddressed by AI-native companies. Every grid operator in the world is running dispatch systems built before machine learning existed.

### 4. India's Renewable Buildout Is the Largest Infrastructure Program on Earth

India's renewable energy pipeline as of early 2026 totals 486.94 GW across all stages: 237.43 GW solar, 85.9 GW wind, 70.72 GW hybrid/storage combinations, and 75.68 GW large hydro. The government is targeting 500 GW of non-fossil fuel capacity by 2030 and is adding 50 GW per year to reach it.

In 2025, India added 44.51 GW of new renewable capacity, backed by $22.32 billion in investment — both records. Solar drove the growth: 34.98 GW installed in 2025, bringing cumulative solar to 132.85 GW. Despite this record year, Global Energy Monitor's analysis shows India needs to double its deployment pace to meet the 2030 target. That gap between current pace and required pace is an investment opportunity, not a failure.

Maharashtra — the state containing Pune — is specifically identified as having among the highest solar potential in India, with planned hybrid and solar-plus-storage locations and transmission schemes under active implementation.

### 5. India's Carbon Market Is Launching — and It's Bigger Than Most Realize

India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) formally transitioned nine industrial sectors — aluminium, cement, steel, fertilisers, chlor-alkali, pulp and paper, petrochemicals, petroleum refining, and textiles — in FY2026. These nine sectors represent approximately 16% of India's total emissions.

In March 2025, the Bureau of Energy Efficiency released Version 1 of the Offset Mechanism procedures, approving eight methodologies covering renewable energy, green hydrogen, industrial efficiency, landfill methane, mangrove afforestation, and offshore wind. The full Indian Carbon Market — compliance plus voluntary offset mechanisms — is expected to launch officially by mid-2026.

This is not the EU ETS, but it is the infrastructure for one. The monitoring, reporting, and verification (MRV) ecosystem that needs to be built around it is a massive, underserved opportunity.

### 6. Industrial Decarbonization Is Happening Faster Than Predicted

Steel, cement, chemicals, and shipping collectively represent roughly 30% of global emissions — the "hard to abate" sectors that electrification cannot easily reach. Three things have changed in 2025:

First, green hydrogen electrolyzer costs fell from $250/kW in early 2024 to under $100/kW by 2025 due to manufacturing scale and competitive oversupply — making green hydrogen cost trajectories credible for the first time. Second, carbon border adjustment mechanisms in Europe are creating financial pressure on export-oriented Indian industries to measure and reduce Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions or pay a carbon tariff. Third, SEBI's BRSR Core framework has made ESG data disclosure mandatory for India's top 500 listed companies in FY2025-26, creating demand for measurement and reporting infrastructure that did not exist at this scale previously.

### 7. The ESG Compliance Deadline Is Creating a Software Market Overnight

SEBI's BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting) framework is now mandatory for India's top 500 listed companies, with value chain disclosures required for the top 250 from FY2025-26. The regulation covers energy consumption, water usage, emissions intensity, supply chain sustainability, and board-level accountability.

Most Indian companies have no system of record for this data. They are managing it in spreadsheets — if at all. The compliance deadline is real, the penalties for non-compliance are escalating, and the software tools to automate data collection, calculation, and reporting are nascent. This is a textbook software market creation event: external mandate, clear deadline, no dominant incumbent, and a customer base willing to pay to avoid regulatory risk.

## The Companies Shaping the Future

### The Energy Transition

**Tesla Energy**
Tesla deployed 12.5 GWh of energy storage in Q3 2025 — up 81% YoY — and crossed the milestone of 1 million Powerwall units installed. The Megapack 3 and Megablock system, unveiled September 2025, delivers 20 MWh in a single integrated unit installable in one week. The Shanghai Megapack factory commenced production in early 2025. Tesla's Lathrop, California facility alone is capable of producing 10,000 Megapacks annually (40 GWh of capacity). Tesla Energy is now a significant profit center independent of vehicle sales — and its margin profile improves every year as manufacturing learning curves compound.

**Ørsted**
The world's largest offshore wind developer with 10.2 GW installed (30 farms) and 8.1 GW under construction targeting 2027 completion. Ørsted generates an estimated 2,000–4,000 MWh into power grids every hour. The company took a brutal write-down ($5.6B) on US offshore wind projects in 2023 when US interest rates spiked — and that pain is now largely behind it, with project economics recalibrating to higher rates. Ørsted proves that offshore wind at scale is achievable, and its European portfolio is generating positive returns at current costs.

**Enphase Energy**
Enphase holds approximately 60% market share in US residential solar microinverters and has maintained 45% domestic share despite California's NEM 3.0 policy changes compressing rooftop economics. What Enphase has built beyond hardware: a software platform that manages residential solar and storage assets as a distributed virtual power plant, enabling demand response at neighborhood scale. The IQ8 microinverter works off-grid without a battery — a meaningful resilience feature as grid outages increase with extreme weather. Revenue was $1.33B in 2024 after NEM 3.0 headwinds; the trajectory recovered through 2025 as storage attachment rates rose.

**First Solar**
The only major US-based solar manufacturer at scale. First Solar uses cadmium telluride (CdTe) thin-film technology — a fundamentally different chemistry from the Chinese silicon supply chain — which is now capturing 30% of the US utility-scale solar market. In November 2025, First Solar announced construction of a new 3.7 GW CdTe factory, targeting initial operations by end of 2026. First Solar matters because it is the primary non-Chinese manufacturing option as trade policy creates incentives for domestic supply chains in the US market.

**LONGi**
The world's largest solar manufacturer by volume. LONGi's HIBC technology, launched at SNEC 2025 with 700W output at 25.9% module efficiency (the first time silicon solar has crossed 25% in commercial production), redefines what a solar panel can do per unit of rooftop area. LONGi set dual world records for both HIBC and tandem solar cell efficiency in 2025, with results published in *Nature*. The efficiency trajectory matters because it means fewer panels needed per megawatt — which changes the economics of land-constrained installations globally.

### The Battery Revolution

**CATL**
Contemporary Amperex Technology sold 661 GWh of lithium-ion batteries in 2025 while reporting profit growth — an extraordinary combination during a year of 40–45% price deflation in battery packs. CATL's scale advantage (it processes more battery chemistry per month than most countries have processed in total) allows it to remain profitable at prices that would bankrupt competitors. CATL's Condensed Battery technology — targeting energy densities above 500 Wh/kg — is aimed at applications (aviation, long-range EVs) that current chemistry cannot serve. If it scales commercially, it opens an entirely new set of use cases.

**QuantumScape**
QuantumScape is closest to commercializing solid-state lithium-metal batteries — a technology that promises 2–3x the energy density of current lithium-ion and eliminates the thermal runaway risk that makes current batteries dangerous. In October 2025, QuantumScape began delivering B1 samples of its QSE-5 cell to Volkswagen: 5 Ah capacity, 844+ Wh/L energy density, charging from 10% to 80% in 12.2 minutes. The Eagle Line pilot production facility inaugurated in early 2026 is designed to demonstrate scalable production ahead of Volkswagen licensing the technology for gigawatt-hour-scale manufacturing. Still pre-revenue, still burning capital — but the technical milestones are being hit.

**Form Energy**
Form Energy's iron-air battery technology does what lithium-ion cannot: provide 100+ hour discharge duration at costs approaching $20/kWh, using iron (the world's cheapest abundant metal) as the storage medium. Iron-air is not fast enough for grid frequency regulation, but for multi-day backup against weather events — exactly the grid resilience problem that offshore wind creates — it is potentially transformative. Form raised $405M in its Series F. The commercial deployment question is the key unknown: manufacturing at scale with a novel electrochemistry is hard, and the timeline from pilot to production is uncertain.

**Ola Electric**
India's largest electric two-wheeler manufacturer, Ola delivered its first electric motorcycle (Roadster series) in 2025 and launched a battery swapping initiative in October 2025 targeting fleet operators and commercial EV users. Ola's Gigafactory is India's most ambitious domestic battery manufacturing project — critical for India's ambition to avoid dependence on Chinese battery supply chains. The IPO of Ola Electric in 2024 gave it a public-market profile and capital to expand the manufacturing base.

**Log9 Materials**
A cautionary case worth understanding. Log9 was India's most prominent deep-tech battery startup — graphene-based batteries, lithium-titanate chemistry, "RapidX" branding for fleet operators. After raising over $60M, Log9 ran into cash flow problems in 2025, with confirmed layoffs and significant operational contraction. The lesson: hardware manufacturing timelines routinely exceed funding runways. India has genuine battery technology talent; the challenge is that battery manufacturing at commercial scale requires capital intensity that most VC portfolios are not structured to support through the valley of death between pilot and production.

### AI for Climate

**Climavision**
Climavision fills the gaps in weather visibility that the US National Weather Service's Doppler radar network misses — particularly below 10,000 feet where the most commercially and operationally critical weather events happen. By combining proprietary low-altitude radar data with AI-driven forecasting, Climavision provides hyperlocal, high-resolution weather intelligence to energy operators, aviation companies, and logistics networks. For renewable energy dispatch operators, sub-hourly, hyper-local wind and irradiance forecasting directly translates into dollars: better forecasts reduce reserve margin requirements and curtailment rates.

**Pachama (acquired by Carbon Direct, November 2025)**
Pachama built the leading AI platform for verifying forest carbon projects — combining satellite imagery, lidar scans, and machine learning to measure actual forest carbon sequestration rather than relying on modeled estimates. This is the measurement layer that the voluntary carbon market desperately needs: rigorous, continuous, satellite-verified baselines that make it hard to sell credits for carbon that was not actually sequestered. Carbon Direct's acquisition of Pachama creates an integrated carbon management and verification platform. The MRV (monitoring, reporting, verification) problem is one of the largest unsolved problems in climate tech, and Pachama's technology is a direct solution.

**Watershed**
Watershed is the enterprise carbon accounting and ESG management platform for large global companies. Named a leader in the Verdantix 2026 Green Quadrant for Enterprise Carbon Management Software, Watershed's pitch is audit-grade data: Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions tracking, regulatory report generation (CSRD, SEC climate rules, BRSR), and decarbonization pathway management. The customer is the Fortune 500 sustainability team trying to meet a mandatory disclosure deadline. The market is every public company in every jurisdiction that has adopted mandatory climate disclosure — which, by 2026, includes the EU, UK, and incrementally, India's BRSR framework.

### Carbon and Capture

**Varaha (India)**
The most important carbon removal company operating in the Global South right now. Founded in 2022, Varaha helps Indian, Bangladeshi, and Nepalese farmers earn carbon credits through regenerative agriculture, biochar production, and nature-based solutions. The company has raised approximately $33M in equity plus $35M in project financing.

The key deals tell the story: Google signed the world's largest biochar carbon removal deal with Varaha in January 2025 — purchasing 100,000 tons of CO2 removal credits from cotton crop waste converted to biochar (the first such deal in India). Microsoft signed a deal in January 2026 for 100,000+ tons of carbon removal credits over three years through 2029, also from Indian agricultural biochar. Varaha then raised $20M in February 2026 as the first tranche of a $45M Series B.

Varaha is also the first company to issue registry-backed Enhanced Rock Weathering (ERW) carbon credits in Asia — a significant technical and commercial first. The company reported ₹430 million (~$4.76M) in revenue from delivered credits and expects to cross ₹1 billion (~$11M) this year while remaining profitable. This is a real business with real revenue from real carbon removal.

**Charm Industrial**
Charm converts agricultural residues into bio-oil through fast pyrolysis, then permanently sequesters that bio-oil underground — a process that removes CO2 from the atmosphere on geological timescales. Charm has sequestered nearly 12,000 tons of CO2 to date, with $100M in funding and a $53M carbon removal deal with the Frontier Fund (a consortium backed by Google, Meta, Shopify, and McKinsey). Charm's technology is expensive per ton — but the permanence of underground bio-oil sequestration is higher than most nature-based solutions.

**Heirloom**
Heirloom uses enhanced mineral weathering on limestone: grinding limestone into powder, spreading it over solar-drying fields, and letting it naturally absorb CO2 — a process that normally takes years, compressed to three days through temperature and surface area optimization. The captured CO2 is then stored permanently in concrete or geological formations. Heirloom has raised $54.3M from investors including Bill Gates. The technology is elegant in its simplicity — no exotic chemistry, no high-temperature processes, just physics operating faster.

**CarbonCure**
CarbonCure injects captured CO2 into concrete during mixing, where it mineralizes permanently — removing carbon and making the concrete stronger simultaneously. Total funding: $92.4M (including an $80M round in 2023). The business model is smart: CarbonCure does not sell concrete, it licenses the technology to concrete producers who pay per ton of CO2 injected. Every concrete pour that uses CarbonCure technology is a permanent carbon sink. The addressable market is roughly 30 billion tons of concrete poured globally per year.

### India's Climate Moment

**Ather Energy**
India's most technically sophisticated electric scooter manufacturer. Ather IPO'd on BSE and NSE in May 2025 at an issue size of ₹2,980 crore, listing on May 6, 2025 — a landmark for India's electric vehicle ecosystem. Ather competes with Ola Electric, TVS, and Bajaj, but has built its reputation on software and charging infrastructure quality rather than price. The Ather Grid charging network and connected vehicle platform represent a differentiated model in a market where most competitors compete purely on hardware price.

**Yulu**
Yulu operates India's largest shared electric micromobility network — electric cycles and mopeds for urban commuters — with Battery-as-a-Service infrastructure enabling battery swapping rather than charging. With over 3.5 million battery swaps to date and plans for 500+ swapping stations, Yulu is building the physical infrastructure for electric last-mile logistics. Its partnership with Bajaj Auto gives it manufacturing and distribution scale. The commercial fleet market — food delivery, logistics, parcel delivery — is Yulu's core customer.

**Fourth Partner Energy**
India's leading commercial and industrial (C&I) solar developer, with over 3,000 projects executed across distributed solar and open access. Fourth Partner is targeting 3 GW of installed capacity and has partnerships with the Asian Development Bank for solar power plant construction serving commercial and industrial customers. The company has foray into energy storage and solar-powered EV charging hubs. Fourth Partner raised $125M in funding and operates in a market — corporate procurement of renewable energy — that is growing directly in proportion to India's manufacturing FDI.

**Kazam**
Kazam is India's fastest-scaling EV charging infrastructure platform — building the software and hardware stack for charge point operators, vehicle OEMs, fleet operators, and utilities. In January 2025, Kazam launched a low-power DC fast charger specifically designed for India's 2W and 3W electric vehicle market (6–12 kW output, affordable for commercial operators). Kazam raised $6.2M in Series B from IFC and Vertex Ventures. Seven to eight major Indian vehicle manufacturers and approximately 30 fleet companies use Kazam's hardware and software. The company is expanding into Southeast Asia.

**Claro Energy**
Claro Energy is addressing a problem most climate-tech investors overlook: agricultural irrigation in rural India runs on diesel pumps. Solar-powered irrigation eliminates diesel operating costs for farmers and removes a significant point source of rural emissions. Claro has installed over 16,850 solar pumps serving more than 100,000 individuals across high-deficit, diesel-dependent, agrarian regions. The PM-KUSUM scheme (Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthan Mahabhiyan) is providing government subsidies to replace diesel pumps with solar — creating a policy tailwind that is effectively building Claro's market.

**ReNew Energy**
India's largest independent renewable power producer, listed on NASDAQ since 2021. As of March 2025, ReNew's portfolio stands at approximately 18.5 GW total (commissioned + pipeline), with 10 GW commissioned — powering around 6 million Indian homes. ReNew holds India's largest wind portfolio at 4.90 GW and 5.7 GW solar across eight states. ReNew represents roughly 10% of India's total solar and wind generation. The company is not a startup — it is the scaled, institutional expression of India's renewable transition.

## What This Means for Founders

### The Solopreneur Opportunities Are Specific

Climate tech has two categories of opportunity: hard and capital-intensive (manufacturing, grid infrastructure, carbon removal at scale), and soft and capital-light (software, services, data). Founders with zero institutional capital can win in the second category right now.

**ESG and BRSR Compliance SaaS**
SEBI's mandatory BRSR reporting now covers India's top 500 listed companies, with the top 250 required to report value chain (Scope 3) emissions from FY2025-26. The top 1,000 are expected to be covered by FY2027. These companies need software to collect energy, water, and emissions data from their operations — data that is currently scattered across ERPs, invoices, and spreadsheets.

The opportunity is not building a generic sustainability platform (Watershed already exists, ESG tree-platform incumbents are established globally). The opportunity is building an India-specific compliance tool: pre-loaded BRSR templates, automated calculation of Indian industry emission factors, native integration with Indian ERP systems (SAP India, Tally, Busy), and report formats aligned with SEBI's specific disclosure requirements. A vertical SaaS for one specific industry — manufacturing, pharma, textiles — is more defensible than a horizontal platform. One person can build an MVP in three months and charge ₹2–5 lakh/year to companies whose compliance penalty for non-filing is far higher.

**Carbon MRV for India's CCTS**
India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme has approved eight offset methodologies. For each methodology, there are obligated monitoring, reporting, and verification workflows. Renewable energy project developers need MRV software. Industrial energy efficiency projects need measurement platforms. Regenerative agriculture projects (Varaha's market) need satellite-integrated monitoring tools.

The MRV software stack for India's domestic carbon market does not exist in a form calibrated to Indian industry structure, Indian agriculture, and Indian regulatory format. This is a classic unbundling opportunity: take what Pachama built for forest carbon in the Americas, rebuild for Indian CCTS methodologies, and sell to the project developers who need BEE registry approval.

**Energy Monitoring and Analytics for MIDC Industrial Clusters**
The Maharashtra Industrial Development Corporation (MIDC) hosts hundreds of industrial clusters across Pune, Aurangabad, Nashik, and Nagpur. These clusters consume enormous amounts of energy, most of it poorly monitored. As BRSR and industrial energy efficiency mandates tighten, MIDC companies need IoT-connected energy monitoring — meter-level data collection, demand forecasting, power factor correction analytics, renewable procurement dashboards.

Pune specifically sits adjacent to Pimpri-Chinchwad MIDC (automotive manufacturing) and Bhosari MIDC (engineering and chemicals). An energy monitoring SaaS built for MIDC industrial customers, offering hardware-software bundles using off-the-shelf IoT sensors and a cloud dashboard, can serve a market of thousands of SME manufacturers within a 50 km radius. This is a physical-digital business that requires some field sales but very little capital to get to first revenue.

**Solopreneur Micro-SaaS: Carbon Footprint for Supply Chains**
European CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism) is now live. Indian exporters in steel, cement, aluminium, fertilisers, and chemicals who export to Europe must provide product-level carbon footprint data or pay a carbon tariff. Most Indian SME exporters in these sectors have no mechanism to calculate product-level emissions.

A solopreneur can build a product-level carbon calculator for Indian exporters: input the production process, energy sources, and material inputs; output an EU-format CBAM declaration document. No complex AI, no hardware, no enterprise sales cycle — just software that automates a compliance workflow that currently costs ₹5–10 lakh per product line in consultant fees.

### The Maharashtra / Pune Angle

Maharashtra's position in India's energy transition is structural, not coincidental. The state has some of the highest solar irradiance in the country. It contains the densest concentration of manufacturing industry outside of Gujarat. It is home to India's largest automotive OEM cluster (Tata, Bajaj, Mahindra, Force Motors — all within 50 km of Pune). The Maharashtra government has a 50 GW renewable energy target with specific execution programs for distributed solar, agricultural solar, and commercial procurement.

This creates a specific founder advantage:

The automotive manufacturing cluster in Pimpri-Chinchwad, Chakan, and Talegaon is under simultaneous pressure from EV transition (which requires retooling) and ESG disclosure mandates (which require energy data). A founder who can serve that cluster with energy monitoring, Scope 1/2 emissions reporting, or EV fleet transition planning has a warm market with a willing-to-pay customer base that is geographically concentrated enough to serve through direct sales.

India's CCTS is building its enforcement infrastructure, and Maharashtra's large industrial base in the nine covered sectors means hundreds of companies in the state will be obligated entities. The compliance software, consulting, and verification market will be built first in the states with the densest industrial concentration. Maharashtra is the largest of those states.

ReNew Energy, Fourth Partner Energy, and the broader C&I solar developer ecosystem are all actively seeking rooftop solar and open-access projects in Maharashtra. This creates a downstream opportunity for installation, asset monitoring, and grid-integration services that do not require competing in the capital-intensive generation development business.

## The Bottom Line

The energy transition is not a slow-moving, policy-dependent story anymore. Solar and battery costs have followed exponential decline curves for fifteen years without interruption. In 2025, utility-scale battery storage dropped below the operating cost of gas peakers. India installed a record 44.51 GW of renewable capacity in a single year. Varaha — an Indian startup founded in 2022 — landed the world's largest biochar carbon removal deal with Google and followed it with a Microsoft contract. LONGi pushed silicon solar efficiency past 25% for the first time in commercial production.

The gap between what analyst models project and what the physical evidence shows has never been wider. The companies moving fastest are not waiting for policy mandates — they are winning on cost, and cost curves do not reverse. For founders in Pune, the opportunity is not building solar panels or grid-scale batteries. It is building the software infrastructure — compliance tools, MRV platforms, energy monitoring systems — that the physical buildout requires but that no one has built yet for India's specific regulatory, industrial, and agricultural context.

The energy transition needs software engineers more than it needs hardware inventors right now. The hardware has already been invented. The data infrastructure has not.
