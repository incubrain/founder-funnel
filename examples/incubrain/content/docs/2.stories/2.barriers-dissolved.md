## The Old Gatekeepers Are Gone

For decades, building technology required the right degree, the right location, the right age, and the right connections. AI has dissolved every single one of these barriers. These stories prove it.

---

### Elly Savatia: Gen Z Kenyan Builds What Google Wouldn't

**Elly Savatia** (Nairobi) built Terp 360, an AI system that translates speech into African sign languages using 3D avatars. She recorded over 2,300 signs herself. Won the Royal Academy of Engineering's Africa Prize ($67K).

Google and Apple had the resources to solve this. They didn't. One person in Nairobi did — because AI gave her the tools to match their engineering capability.

:cited-source{url="https://www.cnn.com/world/africa/speech-to-sign-language-app-wins-africa-innovation-prize-spc" label="CNN"}

---

### Nigerian AI Engineers: Building What Silicon Valley Wouldn't

Three Lagos developers — **Saheed Ayanniyi** (YarnGPT), **Sulaiman Adewale** (Xara), and **Yinka Iyinolakan** (Indigenius AI) — independently built AI tools supporting 180+ African languages.

GPT-4 and Google Translate didn't serve their communities. So they built their own solutions. Geography is no longer a limitation — it's a perspective advantage.

:cited-source{url="https://techcabal.com/2025/12/05/10-exciting-african-ai-products-launched-in-2025/" label="TechCabal"}

---

### Susan Trombetti (Age 60): AI as Chief Strategist

**Susan Trombetti** (60, Washington D.C.) uses AI as her "chief strategist" to run a competitive upscale matchmaking business solo. She competes against app-based dating platforms backed by hundreds of millions in VC funding.

AI adoption among adults aged 50+ nearly doubled between 2024 and 2025 (18% to 30%). Age isn't a barrier — it's an asset when combined with decades of domain expertise.

:cited-source{url="https://www.nextavenue.org/the-secret-advantage-older-entrepreneurs-have-with-ai/" label="Next Avenue"}

---

### "Vibe Coding": Collins Word of the Year 2026

Non-programmers are building functional applications in minutes by describing what they want in plain language. "Vibe coding" became so mainstream it was named Collins Dictionary Word of the Year for 2026.

The barrier between "I have an idea" and "I have a working app" has collapsed. You no longer need to learn to code to build software — you need to learn to think clearly about what you want.

:cited-source{url="https://www.uc.edu/news/articles/2026/03/coding-without-code--how-vibe-coding-rewrites-the-rules.html" label="University of Cincinnati"}
