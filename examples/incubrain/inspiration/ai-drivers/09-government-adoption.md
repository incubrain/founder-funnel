# Government Adoption & Policy: The Legitimacy Engine of AI Progress

> **Research Date:** March 2026
> **Driver Category:** Institutional / Regulatory
> **Thesis:** Government adoption of AI creates a compounding legitimacy-funding-demand cycle that accelerates the entire ecosystem. When governments deploy AI in public services, fund national compute infrastructure, and establish regulatory frameworks, they simultaneously validate the technology for private-sector adoption, create massive demand for AI products, and shape the trajectory of AI development globally. By 2026, government AI policy has become one of the most consequential drivers of AI progress.

## 1. Current State: The Global Landscape of Government AI Strategy (2025-2026)

### How Many Countries Have Formal AI Strategies?

More than **60 countries** have published national AI strategies as of early 2026, with Canada being the first in 2017. The pace of adoption is accelerating: in 2024 alone, **12 new national AI strategies** were published or announced — three times more than in 2023. Most new strategies came from low- and lower-middle-income countries, including Ethiopia, Ghana, Nigeria, Sri Lanka, Uzbekistan, and Zambia.

## 2. China's One-Person Company Program: A Deep Dive

### Overview

China's "one-person company" (OPC) initiative represents a historically unprecedented government-level recognition of AI-powered solopreneurship. Under a national AI push, local governments are converting idle data centers and office spaces into free incubators, offering computing subsidies, equity financing, and housing to solo AI founders. This is not a fringe experiment — it is a coordinated, multi-city, multi-billion-yuan initiative.

Source: [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2026/china-ai-one-person-companies-incentives/), [China Minutes](https://chinaminutes.com/2026/03/06/one-person-companies-in-china/)

### Scale and Growth

- The number of one-person limited liability companies in China exceeded **16 million** nationwide as of June 2025
- **2.86 million** new OPCs were registered in the first half of 2025, surging **47% year-on-year**
- Dozens of OPC-specific communities have appeared across Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, Hangzhou, Suzhou, and Nanjing since September 2025

Source: [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202603/03/WS69a6b610a310d6866eb3b67b.html), [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-02-14/china-bets-on-ai-one-person-companies-to-boost-employment-102414754.html)

### City-by-City Competition

**Shanghai:**
- Launched a **RMB 1 billion ($140 million) subsidy plan** for the AI sector — two-thirds subsidize computing power costs, the rest goes to third-party AI models
- Pudong New Area offers up to **300,000 yuan ($44,000)** in free computing power credits for newly registered solo firms
- Subsidy rates range from 10% to 100% of contract values for computing, AI models, and datasets
- Lin-gang Special Area offers free accommodation, welcome packages, and rent-free startup spaces for up to three years
- The city has committed to building **3,000 new talent apartments** with rent reduced for up to six years
- Xuhui district plans to build five Super Entrepreneur communities with ~2,500 workstations and cultivate 200+ companies by mid-2026

Source: [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3319924/chinas-ai-race-heats-shanghai-launches-massive-subsidy-scheme), [City News Service](https://www.citynewsservice.cn/articles/shanghaidaily/news/one-person-powered-by-ai-shanghai-backs-a-new-entrepreneur-model-zmz880jm)

**Hangzhou:**
- Distributing **250 million yuan** in computing power subsidies
- Nurturing its "six little dragons" — well-known AI startups including DeepSeek
- OPC incubators in state-owned coworking spaces connecting AI startups with potential clients and suppliers
- **1 billion yuan OPC fund** for government-backed capital

Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/02/hangzhou-liangzhu-china-ai-physical-robots-startups-manycore-nvidia-unitree-deep-silicon-valley.html)

**Shenzhen:**
- Longgang District released draft policy (March 7, 2026) with subsidies totaling millions of yuan
- One incubator in Luohu district aims to sponsor more than **50 AI startups**
- Aims to establish **10 nationally leading OPC communities** by end of 2027 and foster **1,000+ high-growth AI startups**

Source: [NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/shenzhen-longgang-backs-openclaw-with-millions-in-subsidies-for-one-person-ai-companies)

**Wuhan:**
- Offering special loans for AI solopreneurs
- Government promises to help cover losses if founders default

**Beijing:**
- "Zero Gravity" OPC community offers founders rent-free workspace for up to three years, with a monthly service fee of just **90 yuan (~$12.50)**

Source: [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2026/china-ai-one-person-companies-incentives/)

### The Incentive Stack

| Incentive | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Housing** | Free apartments, 3,000+ talent apartments, rent-free for up to 3-6 years |
| **Office space** | Free coworking for up to 3 years, converted data centers as incubators |
| **Computing credits** | Up to 300,000 yuan per firm, 3 months free computing, 250M yuan in Hangzhou subsidies |
| **Capital** | 1B yuan Hangzhou OPC fund, government-backed equity financing, Wuhan default coverage |
| **Demonstration grants** | Up to 4 million yuan for demonstration projects |

### Why It Matters

China's OPC program is the first large-scale government recognition that AI enables one person to build what previously required a team. This is not just an employment program — it is an industrial strategy that bets on distributed innovation. If successful, it could produce thousands of AI-native micro-enterprises across every sector of the Chinese economy, creating a competitive advantage through sheer volume of experimentation.

## 3. Regulatory Landscape: Accelerator or Brake?

### The Three Regulatory Philosophies

The world's three largest AI powers have adopted fundamentally different regulatory approaches:

| | **EU** | **US** | **China** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Philosophy** | Risk-based comprehensive regulation | Deregulation + innovation leadership | Targeted rules + pilot programs |
| **Key legislation** | EU AI Act (2024) | Executive Orders (2025) | Cybersecurity Law amendments, AI labeling rules |
| **Approach to risk** | Ex-ante classification | Ex-post enforcement | Sector-specific guidance |
| **Effect on adoption** | Potentially decelerating for SMEs | Accelerating through barrier removal | Balanced acceleration |

### EU AI Act: Comprehensive but Complex

**Timeline:**
- February 2, 2025: Prohibited AI practices and AI literacy obligations applicable
- August 2, 2025: Governance rules and GPAI model obligations applicable
- August 2, 2026: Full applicability
- August 2, 2027: Extended transition for high-risk AI in regulated products

**Key provisions:**
- Prohibition of emotion recognition AI in workplaces
- Mandatory transparency when people interact with AI
- Risk assessment and mitigation requirements for high-risk AI systems
- New labeling and watermarking requirements for AI-generated content

**Proposed simplification:** The Digital Omnibus on AI (November 2025) aims to ease compliance, link high-risk obligation deadlines to standards availability, and extend deadlines to December 2027 and August 2028.

**Net effect:** Mixed. Large, well-resourced organizations may accelerate adoption through clear frameworks. Smaller companies face high compliance costs. Regulatory uncertainty during the transition period creates litigation risk.

Source: [SIG](https://www.softwareimprovementgroup.com/blog/eu-ai-act-summary/), [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-11-24-eu-ai-act-proposed-digital-omnibus-on-ai-will-impact-businesses-ai-compliance-roadmaps)

### US: Deregulation as Strategy

**Key executive orders:**
1. **January 2025:** "Removing Barriers to American Leadership in AI" — rescinded Biden's EO 14110, shifted from oversight to deregulation
2. **July 2025:** AI Action Plan + orders to prevent "woke" AI, accelerate data center permitting, expand AI infrastructure
3. **December 2025:** "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for AI" — established DOJ AI Litigation Task Force to challenge restrictive state AI laws in federal court

**State-level activity:** More than **700 AI-related bills** introduced at the US federal level in 2024, with 2025 starting even faster. At the state level, at least **45 states** proposed AI-related bills in 2024, and **31 states and territories** enacted AI laws or resolutions.

**Net effect:** Strongly accelerating. Removal of federal regulatory barriers plus active preemption of state-level restrictions creates the most permissive AI development environment among major economies.

Source: [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/12/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-ensures-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence/), [Sidley Austin](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/12/unpacking-the-december-11-2025-executive-order)

### China: Targeted Rules Without Comprehensive Law

**Key regulatory actions (2025-2026):**
- **April 2025:** Three national standards for generative AI security (effective November 2025)
- **August 2025:** Draft measures for ethical management of AI technology
- **September 2025:** AI labeling rules became mandatory — AI-generated content must have explicit labels (watermarks) and implicit metadata labels
- **October 2025:** Cybersecurity Law amendments passed, bringing AI into national law for the first time (effective January 2026)
- **Late 2025:** Draft regulation targeting addiction and psychological harms from AI companions/chatbots

**Net effect:** Carefully balanced. China has deliberately removed a comprehensive AI law from its legislative agenda, choosing instead to regulate specific risks while keeping overall compliance costs low. This approach allows rapid deployment while managing targeted concerns.

Source: [IAPP](https://iapp.org/resources/article/global-ai-governance-china), [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/artificial-intelligence-a-brave-new-world-china-formulates-new-ai-global--governance-action-plan-and-issues-draft-ethics-rules-and-ai-labelling-rules)

### Global Regulatory Velocity

- In 2024, **59 AI-related regulations** were introduced by US federal agencies — more than double the 25 in 2023
- AI mentions in legislative proceedings increased **21.3%** across 75 major countries in 2024 (1,889, up from 1,557)
- South Korea passed the Basic AI Law (December 2024, effective January 2026)
- Japan enacted the AI Promotion Act (May 2025) — light-touch regulation encouraging voluntary cooperation

Source: [Stanford HAI AI Index 2025](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report/policy-and-governance), [NAAIA](https://naaia.ai/worldwide-state-of-ai-regulation/)

### Evidence Both Ways: Is Regulation Accelerating or Decelerating Adoption?

**Evidence that regulation accelerates adoption:**
- Clear rules reduce uncertainty for enterprise buyers, unlocking procurement decisions
- Risk frameworks give CIOs cover to deploy AI in sensitive domains (healthcare, finance)
- Regulatory sandboxes (EU, Singapore, UK) create safe spaces for experimentation
- Standards create interoperability, reducing integration costs

**Evidence that regulation decelerates adoption:**
- Compliance costs disproportionately burden startups and SMEs
- Regulatory uncertainty during transition periods freezes investment
- Overly broad definitions (EU "high-risk") capture benign use cases
- Talent diverted from innovation to compliance documentation
- Geographic fragmentation forces companies to maintain multiple compliance regimes

## 5. National AI Compute Infrastructure

### NAIRR (United States)

The **National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR)** is a shared national infrastructure providing researchers with access to computing, software, data, models, and expertise for AI innovation.

**Current status:**
- Transitioned from pilot to laying foundations for a sustainable national program
- Supports over **600 research projects** and **6,000 users**
- Total compute capacity: ~**3.77 exaFLOPS** (equivalent to ~5,000 H100 GPUs)
- Infrastructure: ~27,500 CPUs, 29,000 GPUs, and 6 specialized AI systems
- Includes the Argonne Leadership Computing Facility AI Testbed (Cerebras, Graphcore, Groq, SambaNova systems)

**Operational structure:** NAIRR Open (general access), NAIRR Secure (sensitive data), NAIRR Software (tools/platforms), NAIRR Classroom (education).

The NSF announced a solicitation for the NAIRR Operations Center (NAIRR-OC) to transition from pilot to permanent infrastructure.

Source: [NSF](https://www.nsf.gov/focus-areas/ai/nairr), [NAIRR Pilot](https://nairrpilot.org/), [Georgetown CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/the-nairr-pilot-estimating-compute/)

### EuroHPC AI Factories (European Union)

The European High Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) is building the most ambitious government-funded AI compute network in the world.

**Scale:**
- Over the 2021-2027 period, total EU investment in supercomputing and AI Factories will reach **€10 billion**
- At least **15 AI Factories** and several Antennas being established through 2025-2026
- **13 AI Factory Antennas** selected with ~€55 million EU funding
- Computing capacity expected to **more than double by 2026**

**Deployment waves:**
- December 2024: 7 AI Factories (Finland, Germany, Greece, Italy, Luxembourg, Spain, Sweden)
- March 2025: 6 AI Factories (Austria, Bulgaria, France, Germany, Poland, Slovenia)
- October 2025: 6 AI Factories (Czech Republic, Lithuania, Netherlands, Romania, Spain, Poland)

**Flagship supercomputers** (Top 10 worldwide):
- JUPITER (Germany) — Europe's first exascale supercomputer (ranked 4th globally)
- LUMI (Finland) — ranked 9th
- Leonardo (Italy) — ranked 10th

All systems to be interconnected through a federated platform by mid-2026.

Source: [EuroHPC JU](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/), [Innovation News Network](https://www.innovationnewsnetwork.com/eurohpc-unveils-e1-5bn-funding-for-europes-first-ai-factories/53817/)

### India AI Mission Compute

- Total mission outlay: **Rs 10,372 crore (~$1.24 billion)** over five years
- Achieved **38,000 GPUs** (up from initial 10,000 target)
- 10 companies shortlisted for AI data center GPU deployment
- Budget allocated: Rs 2,000 crore (2025-26), Rs 1,000 crore (2026-27)

Source: [PIB India](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092), [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/india-ai-mission-infrastructure-models/)

### The Sovereign Compute Race

The pattern is clear: nations are treating AI compute as critical infrastructure, on par with energy grids and transportation networks. This "sovereign AI" movement reflects the recognition that dependence on foreign compute creates strategic vulnerability. By 2026, government-funded AI compute has become a key pillar of national security strategy.

Source: [SIAI](https://siai.org/memo/2025/12/202512284707)

## 6. AI in Public Services: Deployments, Successes, and Failures

### Immigration and Border Security (US)

The Department of Homeland Security has deployed AI extensively:
- **29 USCIS-related AI use cases** as of February 2025
- Facial recognition for near-instant identity verification
- The DHS published a Generative AI Public Sector Playbook (January 2025)
- AI deployed across cybersecurity, border security, disaster response, and immigration services

Source: [DHS](https://www.dhs.gov/archive/news/2025/01/07/dhs-unveils-generative-ai-public-sector-playbook), [DHS AI Use Case Inventory](https://www.dhs.gov/ai/use-case-inventory/uscis)

### Healthcare: NHS (UK) and VA (US)

**NHS:**
- AI positioned as a core component of the 10-Year Health Plan for England
- Major trials delivered "unprecedented time and cost savings" (October 2025)
- AI trialled at unprecedented scale across NHS screening (September 2025)
- However, by June 2025, **23 out of 66 hospital trusts** (a third) were not yet using contracted AI tools in clinical practice
- Key barriers: clinical staff workloads, aging IT systems, staff skepticism about AI in healthcare

**VA:**
- Goal of launching **10 more AI use cases** into production in FY 2026
- AI used for document summarization, clinical note drafting, and claims processing

Source: [House of Lords Library](https://lordslibrary.parliament.uk/ai-in-the-nhs/), [EBO Healthcare](https://healthcare.ebo.ai/2026/01/05/ai-and-the-nhs-in-2026-heres-what-to-expect/), [VA AI](https://department.va.gov/ai/building-the-future-vas-strategy-for-adopting-high-impact-artificial-intelligence-to-improve-services-for-veterans/)

### Judicial Systems

**Successes:**
- **Argentina (Prometea):** AI assistant helped legal professionals process **490 cases/month** vs. 130 before — a ~300% productivity increase
- **Brazil (VICTOR):** AI evaluates Supreme Court appeals in seconds vs. 44 minutes for a human clerk
- **China (Smart Courts):** Reduced average trial times by **30%** and significantly decreased clerk workload
- **Croatia (ANON):** AI-powered anonymization of court decisions operationalized January 2025
- **Egypt:** Automated transcription introduced in 2024 to enhance court efficiency

**Failures and concerns:**
- **UK High Court:** Lawyers submitted AI-generated legal arguments citing **non-existent cases**, resulting in fines and delays
- Only **9%** of judicial operators have received any AI-related training, despite **44%** having used AI tools
- Many teams spend more time validating and correcting AI systems they don't trust than experiencing efficiency gains

**Regulatory response:** UNESCO launched Guidelines for AI in Courts and Tribunals (December 2025) with 15 Principles for ethical AI use. US judicial guidelines emphasize that "AI may assist judges but can never replace judicial judgment."

Source: [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-courtroom-unescos-new-guidelines-judiciary), [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/06/governing-with-artificial-intelligence_398fa287/full-report/ai-in-justice-administration-and-access-to-justice_f0cbe651.html), [Stimson Center](https://www.stimson.org/2026/ai-in-global-majority-judicial-systems/)

## 8. Future Direction (2026-2028): Where Government AI Is Heading

### Gartner Predictions

[Gartner's March 2026 prediction](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-17-gartner-predicts-at-least-80-percent-of-governments-will-deploy-ai-agents-to-automate-routine-decision-making-by-2028):
- **80% of governments** will deploy AI agents to automate routine decision-making by 2028
- **70% of government agencies** will require explainable AI (XAI) and human-in-the-loop (HITL) mechanisms by 2029
- **50%** of government respondents cite improved citizen experience as a top-three priority

Key barriers: **41%** cite siloed strategies and **31%** cite legacy systems as challenges to AI implementation.

### From Pilots to Infrastructure

The OECD's landmark 2025 report "Governing with Artificial Intelligence" documents a fundamental shift: public institutions are moving from **isolated pilot projects to large-scale, systemic adoption** of AI across core government functions. AI is becoming "part of the underlying infrastructure that supports decision-making, service delivery, and policy design."

Source: [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/06/governing-with-artificial-intelligence_398fa287.html)

### Government as Major AI Consumer

Several trends point to governments becoming among the largest AI consumers:

1. **Defense procurement:** The Pentagon's $13.4B dedicated AI line item signals sustained, growing demand
2. **Administrative automation:** Workforce reductions (DOGE, IRS cuts) are creating demand for AI to maintain service levels
3. **Healthcare digitization:** NHS, VA, and other health systems are embedding AI as a strategic enabler
4. **Sovereign AI compute:** Government-funded supercomputers create both infrastructure and demand for domestic AI
5. **Education mandates:** Federal AI education investments create pipeline demand for AI tools

### Procurement Transformation

Federal agencies are shifting from cautious experimentation to enterprise-wide AI procurement. Key trends:
- Central IT agencies negotiating AI contracts for all departments
- "Shadow AI" adoption — agencies accessing AI through free pilots, grants, or features built into existing tools
- AI being used to automate procurement itself — evaluating bids, monitoring contract performance

Source: [Open Contracting Partnership](https://www.open-contracting.org/2025/11/10/the-surprising-shifts-in-how-the-public-sector-is-buying-ai-and-what-policymakers-can-do-about-it/)

### 2026 as a Decisive Year

As the [Council on Foreign Relations](https://www.cfr.org/articles/how-2026-could-decide-future-artificial-intelligence) argues, AI is entering a decisive phase "defined less by speculative breakthroughs than by the hard realities of governance, adoption, and strategic competition." Policymakers face mounting pressure to translate abstract principles into enforceable rules as AI moves from experimentation to widespread deployment.

## 9. The Compounding Effect: How Government Adoption Accelerates the Ecosystem

### The Legitimacy-Funding-Demand Cycle

Government adoption of AI creates a self-reinforcing cycle:

```
Government adopts AI
        ↓
Legitimacy signal → Private sector follows ("if the Pentagon uses it, it's real")
        ↓
Market demand grows → More companies build AI products
        ↓
Competition drives innovation → Better, cheaper AI
        ↓
Government adopts more AI (better products, lower cost)
        ↓
Cycle accelerates
```

### Five Compounding Mechanisms

**1. Legitimacy and Trust**
When the NHS deploys AI for cancer screening, when the Pentagon allocates $13.4B for AI, when courts use AI for case management — these actions signal to every organization that AI is ready for serious work. Government adoption removes the "too risky" objection from enterprise procurement committees.

**2. Demand Creation**
Government AI spending ($26.4B in 2025, projected to reach $91-135B by 2034-2035) creates a massive, reliable demand floor. Defense contractors, healthcare IT vendors, and govtech startups all respond to government requirements, building capabilities that spill over into commercial markets.

**3. Talent and Workforce Development**
Government AI education initiatives (White House AI Task Force, $169M FIPSE investment, 53 state-level AI education bills) create the next generation of AI practitioners. India's 38,000 GPU deployment trains researchers who later work in private sector. Singapore's S$1B NAIRD develops AI talent for the broader economy.

**4. Infrastructure as Platform**
Government-funded compute (NAIRR, EuroHPC, IndiaAI) provides researchers and startups with resources they couldn't afford independently. This democratizes AI development — a university researcher on NAIRR can access 29,000 GPUs. Europe's federated AI Factory network creates shared infrastructure across 15+ countries.

**5. Standards and Interoperability**
Government regulation, for all its friction, creates standards that reduce market fragmentation. The EU AI Act's risk classification system is being adopted as a de facto global standard. China's AI labeling requirements push the entire industry toward transparency. These standards reduce integration costs and create larger addressable markets.

### The Strategic Competition Multiplier

Government AI adoption is intensified by geopolitical competition. The US, China, and EU are locked in a race where each government's AI investment triggers reciprocal investment by rivals:

- US allocates $13.4B for defense AI → China accelerates military AI programs → US increases further
- China subsidizes 16M one-person AI companies → US and EU respond with startup incentives
- EU builds €10B compute infrastructure → US expands NAIRR → India scales to 38,000 GPUs

This dynamic creates an upward spiral of government AI investment that is unlikely to reverse regardless of any individual government's policy changes.

### The Accumulated Expertise Effect

As [Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/research/the-ai-adoption-gap) observes, "successfully leveraging AI translates into profits and accumulated expertise, which in turn make it easier to leverage AI in the future — a compounding effect." Governments that adopt AI early accumulate institutional knowledge that makes subsequent adoption faster and more effective. Those that delay face a growing capability gap.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Government AI is no longer experimental.** With 60+ national strategies, $26.4B in public sector spending, and 43% of government employees using AI, the era of "pilot projects" is over. Governments are embedding AI as core infrastructure.

2. **China's OPC program is a paradigm shift.** Government-level recognition that AI enables one-person companies — backed by billions in subsidies, free housing, computing credits, and equity financing — represents the first industrial policy designed for the AI-solopreneur era. 16 million OPCs and counting.

3. **The three regulatory approaches are diverging.** The US deregulates to accelerate innovation. The EU builds comprehensive risk frameworks. China uses targeted rules with low compliance costs. Each creates different opportunities and risks for AI builders.

4. **Defense spending is the largest government AI demand driver.** The Pentagon's $13.4B dedicated AI budget line (FY 2026) is the single largest government AI investment. Defense AI procurement creates technology spillovers into civilian sectors.

5. **Sovereign compute is the new arms race.** NAIRR (US), EuroHPC (€10B), IndiaAI (38,000 GPUs), Singapore (S$1B) — nations are treating AI compute as critical infrastructure. Government-funded compute democratizes access for researchers and startups.

6. **AI in public services shows both promise and peril.** Argentina's Prometea achieves 300% productivity gains in courts. Brazil's VICTOR processes appeals in seconds. But the NHS struggles with adoption in a third of hospital trusts, and IRS algorithms show racial bias in audit selection.

7. **80% of governments will deploy AI agents by 2028.** Gartner's prediction signals that routine government decision-making — permits, benefits, compliance checks — will be increasingly automated, with human-in-the-loop requirements for high-stakes decisions.

8. **The compounding effect is real and accelerating.** Government adoption creates legitimacy (private sector follows), demand (market grows), talent (workforce develops), infrastructure (shared compute), and standards (markets expand). Each effect reinforces the others.

9. **2026 is the inflection year.** The EU AI Act reaches full applicability, China's Cybersecurity Law AI amendments take effect, US AI Growth Zones launch, Saudi Arabia declares the "Year of AI," and government AI agents move from concept to deployment. Policy decisions made in 2026 will shape AI trajectories for a decade.

10. **Government is becoming an AI accelerant, not just a regulator.** The traditional framing of government as either enabling or restricting AI misses the bigger picture: governments are simultaneously the largest AI customers, the biggest compute infrastructure builders, the primary talent pipeline investors, and the standard-setters for the entire ecosystem. This multi-role position makes government adoption one of the most consequential drivers of AI progress.
