## 1. Current State: AI's Energy Consumption (2025-2026)

### Global Data Center Power Consumption

The scale of energy consumed by data centers — increasingly dominated by AI workloads — has entered a new order of magnitude:

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 (Projected) | 2026 (Projected) | 2030 (Projected) |
|--------|------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Global data center electricity (TWh)** | ~415 | ~448 | 650–1,050 | ~945 (IEA Base Case) |
| **% of global electricity** | ~1.5% | ~1.7% | ~2.0–2.5% | ~3.0% |
| **AI-optimized server electricity (TWh)** | — | ~93 | ~130–150 | ~432 |
| **AI share of data center power** | ~10% | ~21% | ~25–30% | ~44% |

Sources: [IEA Energy and AI Report](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai), [Gartner Data Center Electricity Forecast](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-11-17-gartner-says-electricity-demand-for-data-centers-to-grow-16-percent-in-2025-and-double-by-2030), [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/electric-power/041025-global-data-center-power-demand-to-double-by-2030-on-ai-surge-iea)

### Key Statistics

- **AI-optimized servers' electricity usage is set to rise nearly fivefold**, from 93 TWh in 2025 to 432 TWh in 2030 ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai)).
- **In the United States**, data centers are anticipated to represent **6% (or 260 TWh) of total electricity consumption** by 2026 ([Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/10/24/what-we-know-about-energy-use-at-us-data-centers-amid-the-ai-boom/)).
- **Gartner projects** electricity demand for data centers will grow 16% in 2025 alone, and **double by 2030** ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-11-17-gartner-says-electricity-demand-for-data-centers-to-grow-16-percent-in-2025-and-double-by-2030)).
- By 2035, the IEA Base Case sees global data center electricity consumption rising to approximately **1,200 TWh** — roughly equivalent to Japan's entire national electricity consumption ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary)).

### Frontier Model Training Power

The energy required to train cutting-edge AI models reveals the exponential trajectory:

- **GPT-5 training** consumed an estimated **55–60 GWh** of electricity — comparable to the monthly energy consumption of a city of 100,000 people. Training required roughly 50,000 NVIDIA H100 GPUs running for 90 days ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/artificial-intelligence/openai-chatgpt/gpt-5-is-powerful-but-hungry-1-5-million-us-households-energy)).
- **GPT-5 daily inference** may consume up to **45 GWh per day**, enough to power 1.5 million US homes ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/artificial-intelligence/openai-chatgpt/gpt-5-is-powerful-but-hungry-1-5-million-us-households-energy)).
- **The power required to train frontier models is doubling annually**, on trend to reach multiple gigawatts by 2030 ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/blog/power-demands-of-frontier-ai-training)).
- Frontier training runs could require **1–2 GW sustained power by 2028** and **4–16 GW by 2030** ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/blog/power-demands-of-frontier-ai-training)).

## 2. The Power Constraint: Is Energy the Binding Limit?

### Evidence That Energy Is the Binding Constraint

Power availability has overtaken chip supply as the dominant constraint on AI expansion. The evidence is substantial:

**Grid capacity exhaustion:**
- **40% of AI data centers will face operational constraints by 2027** due to power limitations ([EnkiAI](https://enkiai.com/ai-market-intelligence/ai-grid-constraints-2026-the-power-problem-halting-ai)).
- New data center projects exceeding **100 MW face connection delays of five years or more** in key markets ([EnkiAI](https://enkiai.com/ai-market-intelligence/ai-grid-constraints-2026-the-power-problem-halting-ai)).
- As of June 2025, **more than 36 data center projects representing $162 billion in investment were blocked or significantly delayed** due to power constraints ([Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/hyperscalers/hyperscalers-in-2026-what-s-next-for-the-world-s-largest-data-center-operators-)).
- The US power grid, built primarily in the 1960s–1970s, **was not designed for the concentrated industrial loads** AI creates ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/electricity-supply-bottleneck-us-ai-dominance)).

**Demand trajectory:**
- AI is driving an unprecedented surge in global electricity demand — **set to rise more than 1 trillion kWh per year through 2030** ([MacroMicro](https://en.macromicro.me/blog/outlook-2026-series-iv-the-ai-power-endgame-the-infrastructure-race-from-chips-to-the-grid)).
- Data centers alone are expected to account for **nearly 20% of global electricity demand growth** ([MacroMicro](https://en.macromicro.me/blog/outlook-2026-series-iv-the-ai-power-endgame-the-infrastructure-race-from-chips-to-the-grid)).
- **Power constraints are expected to become critical by 2027–2028** due to decades of grid underinvestment and potential supply chain disruptions ([Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/operations-and-management/2026-predictions-ai-sparks-data-center-power-revolution)).

**Geographic concentration:**
- Since 2025, grid-scale data center loads have **exhausted local capacity in primary markets**, triggering connection moratoriums and project freezes ([EnkiAI](https://enkiai.com/ai-market-intelligence/ai-grid-constraints-2026-the-power-problem-halting-ai)).
- Developers are now **prioritizing locations near large-scale power generation** (nuclear plants, renewable zones), fundamentally altering the geographic distribution of AI infrastructure ([EnkiAI](https://enkiai.com/ai-market-intelligence/ai-grid-constraints-2026-the-power-problem-halting-ai)).

### Regions Facing the Most Acute Power Shortages

| Region | Status | Key Challenge |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| **Northern Virginia** | Critical | World's largest data center cluster; local grid nearing capacity |
| **Dublin, Ireland** | Moratorium | Data centers consuming ~21% of Ireland's electricity; new connections restricted |
| **Singapore** | Restricted | Strict PUE mandates (< 1.3); limited new approvals |
| **Amsterdam** | Moratorium | Grid operator TenneT restricting new large connections |
| **US Midwest** | Emerging stress | Rapid new buildout straining regional grids |

### Evidence Against Energy as a Hard Constraint

While energy is clearly the primary bottleneck, several counterarguments deserve consideration:

- **Efficiency gains are real:** Each new GPU generation delivers 3–6x more AI throughput per watt, partially offsetting demand growth.
- **Distributed computing:** Techniques like federated learning and edge AI reduce the need for centralized mega-clusters.
- **AI for energy optimization:** AI itself is being deployed to optimize grid management and energy efficiency, potentially creating a self-correcting mechanism.
- **Alternative power sources:** Nuclear, geothermal, and eventually fusion provide pathways to abundant clean baseload power.

However, these counterarguments are outpaced by demand growth. The consensus among industry analysts is that **energy is and will remain the primary physical constraint on AI scaling through at least 2030**.

## 3. Nuclear Renaissance for AI

Big Tech's pivot to nuclear energy represents one of the most dramatic energy developments of the decade. In aggregate, **tech companies signed over 10 GW of new US nuclear capacity** in the past year alone ([Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/amazon-google-meta-and-microsoft-go-nuclear/)).

### Microsoft: Three Mile Island Restart

- **Deal:** 20-year Power Purchase Agreement with Constellation Energy worth an estimated **$16 billion** to restart Three Mile Island Unit 1 (now renamed the Christopher M. Crane Clean Energy Center).
- **Capacity:** 835 MW of carbon-free baseload power.
- **Timeline:** Targeted restart by **2028**.
- **Federal support:** The US Department of Energy closed a **$1 billion federal loan** to Constellation Energy to support the restart (November 2025).
- **Significance:** First time a retired US nuclear reactor is being brought back to life to serve a single corporate client.

Sources: [Introl Blog](https://introl.com/blog/nuclear-power-ai-data-centers-microsoft-google-amazon-2025), [NucNet](https://www.nucnet.org/news/constellation-secures-usd1-billion-federal-loann-for-three-mile-island-restart-11-3-2025)

### Amazon Web Services: Nuclear Portfolio

- **Susquehanna deal:** 17-year PPA with Talen Energy for **1.92 GW** from the Susquehanna nuclear power plant in Pennsylvania.
- **SMR investment:** **$500 million** invested in Small Modular Reactor development with X-energy.
- **X-energy SMR timeline:** Construction expected to begin in **2026**, with SMRs operational by **2030**.
- **Nuclear share:** Nuclear represented nearly **23% of Amazon's PPA activity** in 2025.

Sources: [Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/energy/article/55239739/data-center-nuclear-power-update-microsoft-constellation-aws-talen-meta), [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/amazon-meta-google-microsoft-account-for-half-of-global-clean-energy-purchase-deals-in-2025-report/)

### Meta: Nuclear-Powered AI

- **Constellation deal:** 20-year PPA for **1,100 MW** from the Clinton, Illinois nuclear power plant, beginning June 2027.
- **Nuclear RFP:** Issued request for proposals targeting **1 to 4 GW** of new nuclear generation.
- **Nuclear share:** Nuclear represented nearly **23% of Meta's PPA activity** in 2025.
- **Rare bee setback:** An early plan for a nuclear-powered data center was shelved after discovery of a rare bee species on the proposed site.

Sources: [Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/energy/article/55239739/data-center-nuclear-power-update-microsoft-constellation-aws-talen-meta), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/11/05/meta-ai-data-center-mark-zuckerberg-nuclear-power-amazon-microsoft-three-mile-island-google/)

- **Kairos Power deal:** First US corporate SMR fleet agreement for **500 MW**, targeting **2030+** deployment.
- **Geothermal strategy:** Partnership with Fervo Energy for **115 MW** of enhanced geothermal in Nevada, plus **150 MW** of conventional geothermal from Ormat Technologies.
- **Fervo Energy investment:** Participated in Fervo's **$462 million** funding round (December 2025).

## 5. Cooling Innovation: Managing Extreme Thermal Density

### The Cooling Crisis

AI chips generate dramatically more heat than traditional servers, making cooling one of the most critical infrastructure challenges:

- Average data center rack power density **increased 38% from 2022 to 2024** ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cooling/the-data-center-cooling-state-of-play-2025-liquid-cooling-is-on-the-rise-thermal-density-demands-skyrocket-in-ai-data-centers-and-tsmc-leads-with-direct-to-silicon-solutions)).
- Power densities that once maxed at **15 kW per rack now push 80–120 kW** in AI clusters, with NVIDIA's upcoming Rubin Ultra targeting **600 kW per rack** ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cooling/the-data-center-cooling-state-of-play-2025-liquid-cooling-is-on-the-rise-thermal-density-demands-skyrocket-in-ai-data-centers-and-tsmc-leads-with-direct-to-silicon-solutions), [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/nvidia-shows-off-rubin-ultra-with-600-000-watt-kyber-racks-and-infrastructure-coming-in-2027)).
- GPU thermal design power (TDP) is projected to **exceed 4,000 W by 2029** ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cooling/the-data-center-cooling-state-of-play-2025-liquid-cooling-is-on-the-rise-thermal-density-demands-skyrocket-in-ai-data-centers-and-tsmc-leads-with-direct-to-silicon-solutions)).

### Liquid Cooling: From Bleeding Edge to Baseline

2025 marked the tipping point where liquid cooling moved from experimental to standard:

**Market size:**
- The liquid cooling market reached **~$3 billion in 2025** (roughly doubling from 2024) and is projected to reach **$7 billion by 2029** ([Dell'Oro Group](https://www.delloro.com/news/data-center-liquid-cooling-market-to-approach-7-billion-by-2029-as-ai-deployments-accelerate/)).
- Broader estimates place the market at **$5.52 billion in 2025**, growing to **$15.75 billion by 2030** (CAGR of 23.3%) ([AIRSYS](https://airsysnorthamerica.com/data-center-trends-cooling-strategies-to-watch-in-2026/)).

**Technology types:**
| Cooling Technology | Heat Flux Capacity | Use Case |
|-------------------|-------------------|----------|
| **Air cooling** | ~10 W/cm² | Traditional servers, low-density racks |
| **Direct-to-chip (cold plate)** | ~250–300 W/cm² | Current GPU clusters (Blackwell, H100) |
| **Single-phase immersion** | ~250 W/cm² | High-density AI training clusters |
| **Two-phase immersion** | ~1,500 W/cm² | Next-gen ultra-dense deployments |

**Hyperscaler deployments:**
- **Google, Meta, AWS, and Microsoft** are rolling out liquid-cooled environments across their newest facilities.
- **Microsoft** tested two-phase immersion cooling for AI training clusters, reporting a **30% energy efficiency gain** and increased hardware reliability ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/analysis/chilling-out-in-2025-a-year-in-data-center-cooling/)).

**Key acquisitions (2025):**
- **Eaton** acquired Boyd Thermal for **$9.5 billion**.
- **Trane** purchased Stellar Energy Digital.
- **Daikin** acquired Chilldyne.
- **Vertiv** acquired PurgeRite for **$1 billion** and is developing an 800 VDC power and cooling portfolio aligned with NVIDIA's roadmap for H2 2026.

Sources: [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/analysis/chilling-out-in-2025-a-year-in-data-center-cooling/), [Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/cooling/article/55292167/liquid-cooling-comes-to-a-boil-tracking-data-center-investment-innovation-and-infrastructure-at-the-2025-midpoint)

### Microsoft's Renewable Strategy

- **Brookfield deal:** Largest single corporate PPA in history — **10.5 GW** of new renewable energy capacity in the US and Europe between 2026 and 2030.
- **Wyoming wind farm:** Data center powered entirely by wind energy.
- **Helion fusion PPA:** World's first fusion power purchase agreement — **50 MW by 2028** (ambitious early target).

Source: [EnkiAI](https://enkiai.com/ai-market-intelligence/microsofts-energy-strategy-2025-fueling-the-ai-boom)

### Geothermal: The 24/7 Clean Baseload Play

Geothermal energy is emerging as a uniquely suited power source for AI data centers because it provides constant, carbon-free baseload power — unlike intermittent solar and wind:

- **Fervo Energy Cape Station:** Expected to deliver **100 MW** in 2026 (Phase 1), scaling to **500 MW by 2028** ([Fervo Energy](https://fervoenergy.com/fervo-uipa-the-enhanced-geothermal-data-center-corridor-july-2025/)).
- **Drilling efficiency:** Fervo completed a well in **16 days** in summer 2025, demonstrating rapid cost reduction ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/10/06/1124292/2025-climate-tech-companies-to-watch-fervo-energy-advanced-geothermal-power-plants/)).
- **Data center corridor:** Fervo is developing an "Enhanced Geothermal Data Center Corridor" — purpose-built geothermal power for AI facilities ([Fervo Energy](https://fervoenergy.com/fervo-uipa-the-enhanced-geothermal-data-center-corridor-july-2025/)).
- **Rhodium Group analysis:** By 2030, enhanced geothermal could provide **nearly two-thirds of new data center demand** at or below current operator prices ([Fervo Energy](https://fervoenergy.com/fervo-uipa-the-enhanced-geothermal-data-center-corridor-july-2025/)).

### Meta's Louisiana Mega-Project

Meta is developing a **$10 billion AI-focused data center** on a 2,250-acre site in Louisiana, with commitments to add at least **1,500 MW of new renewable energy** to the grid — establishing a new model for large-scale AI infrastructure that brings its own power.

## 7. Efficiency Improvements: Doing More with Less

### Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) Trends

PUE measures total facility power divided by IT equipment power — a PUE of 1.0 would mean zero overhead. Trends show steady improvement:

| Metric | Industry Average | Hyperscale Leaders | Google (Q1 2025) |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|------------------|
| **PUE** | 1.56 | 1.09–1.20 | **1.08** (quarterly) / 1.09 (TTM) |

Sources: [Google Data Centers](https://datacenters.google/efficiency/), [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1229367/data-center-average-annual-pue-worldwide/)

- **Singapore** mandates PUE under **1.3** for new builds, along with water usage effectiveness targets ([DCNT Global](https://www.dcntglobal.com/top-10-data-center-construction-trends-in-2026/)).
- 2026 construction priorities include **low-carbon materials, efficient electrical and cooling systems, and renewable integration** ([DCNT Global](https://www.dcntglobal.com/top-10-data-center-construction-trends-in-2026/)).

### Chip Architecture Efficiency Gains

Each GPU generation delivers dramatically more AI performance per watt:

| GPU Architecture | TDP | AI Performance | Efficiency vs. Previous |
|-----------------|-----|----------------|------------------------|
| **NVIDIA A100 (Ampere)** | 400W | 312 TFLOPS (FP16) | Baseline |
| **NVIDIA H100 (Hopper)** | 700W | 990 TFLOPS (FP16) | ~1.8x perf/watt |
| **NVIDIA B200 (Blackwell)** | 1,000–1,200W | 18 PFLOPS (FP4) | ~3–5x throughput/watt vs. Hopper |
| **NVIDIA Vera Rubin** (H2 2026) | TBD | TBD | ~4x GPUs/rack, ~6x perf |
| **NVIDIA Rubin Ultra** (2027) | 600kW/rack | 15 EFLOPS (FP4) per rack | Further gains |

- **NVIDIA Blackwell** provides **3–5x more AI throughput in a power-limited data center** compared to Hopper ([NVIDIA](https://www.theregister.com/2024/03/18/nvidia_turns_up_the_ai/)).
- **Google's Trillium (TPU v6)** is **67% more energy-efficient** than TPU v5e ([The Network Installers](https://thenetworkinstallers.com/blog/data-center-energy-consumption-statistics/)).
- Blackwell GPUs are "**25x more energy efficient than traditional CPUs**" for certain AI/HPC workloads ([AMAX](https://www.amax.com/top-5-considerations-for-deploying-nvidia-blackwell/)).

### Software-Level Energy Optimization

- **Model distillation and quantization** reduce inference energy by 2–10x while maintaining accuracy.
- **Mixture-of-experts (MoE)** architectures activate only relevant subsets of parameters, reducing compute per query.
- **Dynamic scaling** adjusts compute allocation based on query complexity.
- **AI-driven datacenter management** can reduce cooling energy by 20–40% (Google DeepMind's data center optimization).

### The Efficiency Paradox (Jevons Paradox)

Despite dramatic per-FLOP efficiency gains, **total energy consumption continues to rise** because:
1. Lower cost per computation drives higher demand.
2. New AI capabilities create entirely new use cases.
3. Model sizes continue to grow faster than efficiency improvements.
4. Inference demand scales with adoption (billions of daily queries).

## 8. Water Consumption: The Hidden Resource Cost

While energy dominates headlines, AI's water consumption is a growing concern:

### Current Consumption

- US data centers consumed **66 billion liters of water in 2023**, up from 21.2 billion liters in 2014 — a **3x increase** in under a decade ([EESI](https://www.eesi.org/articles/view/data-centers-and-water-consumption)).
- Globally, data centers consume approximately **560 billion liters of water annually** ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/ai-data-centers-and-water/)).
- Large individual data centers can consume up to **5 million gallons per day** — equivalent to a town of 10,000–50,000 people ([ELI](https://www.eli.org/vibrant-environment-blog/ais-cooling-problem-how-data-centers-are-transforming-water-use)).

### Projections

- US data center water consumption could **double or quadruple by 2028** to 150–280 billion liters per year ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/ai-data-centers-and-water/)).
- In Texas alone, data centers will use an estimated **49 billion gallons in 2025**, potentially rising to **399 billion gallons by 2030** ([Stanford](https://andthewest.stanford.edu/2025/thirsty-for-power-and-water-ai-crunching-data-centers-sprout-across-the-west/)).
- Water used for cooling may increase by **870%** as more facilities come online ([Net Zero Insights](https://netzeroinsights.com/resources/how-ai-intensifying-data-center-water-consumption/)).

### Environmental Justice Concerns

- **Two-thirds of new data centers** built or in development globally since 2022 are located in places already facing **water stress** ([Lincoln Institute](https://www.lincolninst.edu/publications/land-lines-magazine/articles/land-water-impacts-data-centers/)).
- Training GPT-3 directly evaporated an estimated **700,000 liters of freshwater** in Microsoft's US data centers ([MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2025/explained-generative-ai-environmental-impact-0117)).
- Northern Virginia data centers consumed **close to 2 billion gallons in 2023**, a **63% increase from 2019** ([Undark](https://undark.org/2025/12/16/ai-data-centers-water/)).

## 11. The Compounding Effect: Energy-AI Feedback Loop

The relationship between energy, compute, and AI capability creates a powerful compounding cycle:

```
More Energy Available
       ↓
More Compute Capacity
       ↓
More Capable AI Models
       ↓
More Demand for AI Services
       ↓
More Revenue to Fund Infrastructure
       ↓
More Energy Investment
       ↓
(Cycle Repeats)
```

### The Virtuous Cycle

1. **Energy unlocks compute:** Each new power source (nuclear PPA, geothermal plant, renewable farm) directly translates to more GPU-hours available for training and inference.

2. **Compute unlocks capability:** More compute enables larger models, longer training runs, and more complex reasoning — directly producing more capable AI.

3. **Capability unlocks demand:** More capable AI models create new use cases (coding agents, scientific discovery, autonomous systems), driving adoption and revenue.

4. **Demand unlocks investment:** Higher revenue justifies the $660B+ in annual CapEx, funding more data centers, more power generation, and more cooling infrastructure.

5. **AI optimizes energy:** AI itself is being applied to optimize energy grids, improve drilling efficiency (geothermal), design better reactor systems, and manage data center power — potentially accelerating the supply side.

### The Numbers Behind the Cycle

- **Global AI spending** projected at **$1.5 trillion in 2025**, exceeding **$2 trillion by 2026** ([WEF](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/12/ai-energy-nexus-ai-future/)).
- **Large tech companies committed more than $1 trillion** in spending in just the 2025–2026 period ([Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/powering-ai-energy-market-outlook-2026)).
- Power availability has **overtaken chip supply** as the dominant constraint — meaning solving energy directly unlocks scaling ([EBC Financial](https://www.ebc.com/forex/ai-infrastructure-and-energy-supercycle-market-outlook-2026)).

### AI for Energy: The Self-Reinforcing Dynamic

AI is not just consuming energy — it is increasingly used to **optimize energy systems**:

- **Grid optimization:** AI models predict demand patterns and optimize distribution.
- **Geothermal drilling:** AI-guided drilling reduces well completion time (Fervo's 16-day well).
- **Nuclear design:** AI accelerates reactor design and safety modeling for SMRs.
- **Renewable forecasting:** AI improves solar/wind output predictions, enabling better grid integration.
- **Data center efficiency:** DeepMind-style AI can reduce cooling energy by 20–40%.

However, efficiency gains from AI-driven optimization are limited. Theoretical models suggest **at most a 20% reduction in gross energy demand** through such techniques ([WEF](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/12/ai-energy-nexus-ai-future/)) — far outpaced by demand growth.

### The Risk: The Vicious Cycle

If energy constraints are not addressed, the cycle reverses:

```
Insufficient Energy
       ↓
Compute Scarcity
       ↓
Slower AI Progress
       ↓
Reduced Investment Returns
       ↓
Lower CapEx Appetite
       ↓
Less Infrastructure Build
       ↓
(Stagnation)
```

This is why energy is not just an operational concern — it is an **existential constraint** on the AI industry's growth trajectory.

## 12. Key Takeaways

### The Scale

1. **AI data centers will consume ~945 TWh by 2030** — doubling from 2024 levels and representing ~3% of global electricity. AI-optimized servers alone will consume ~432 TWh, up from 93 TWh in 2025.

2. **Hyperscalers will spend $660–690 billion on infrastructure in 2026** — a 36% increase over 2025. Roughly 75% targets AI specifically. This is the largest capital expenditure program in human history.

3. **The Stargate Project alone represents $500 billion** committed to AI infrastructure, with nearly 7 GW of planned capacity and 10 GW targeted.

### The Constraint

4. **Energy has overtaken chips as the binding constraint** on AI scaling. 40% of AI data centers face operational constraints by 2027. Projects over 100 MW face 5+ year connection delays.

5. **Frontier training runs will require 1–2 GW of sustained power by 2028** — equivalent to a large nuclear power plant running continuously. This doubles annually.

### The Solutions

6. **Nuclear is back.** Big Tech signed 10+ GW of nuclear capacity in one year. Microsoft's Three Mile Island restart (835 MW by 2028), Amazon's 1.92 GW Susquehanna deal, Meta's 1.1 GW Clinton deal, and Google's 500 MW Kairos SMR fleet represent a nuclear renaissance driven by AI demand.

7. **Geothermal is the sleeper.** Fervo Energy's Enhanced Geothermal Data Center Corridor could provide 24/7 carbon-free baseload power, with 500 MW coming online by 2028. By 2030, geothermal could serve two-thirds of new data center demand at competitive prices.

8. **Cooling innovation is critical.** The liquid cooling market doubled in 2025 to ~$3B and will reach $7B by 2029. With rack densities heading from 120 kW to 600 kW (Rubin Ultra), liquid cooling has shifted from optional to mandatory.

### The Dynamics

9. **The energy-AI feedback loop is self-reinforcing.** More energy enables more compute, which produces more capable AI, which drives more demand, which justifies more investment in energy — creating a compounding cycle that accelerates both AI progress and energy innovation.

10. **The environmental costs are real.** Water consumption could quadruple by 2028. xAI's unpermitted gas turbines highlight the tension between AI scaling speed and environmental responsibility. Two-thirds of new data centers are in water-stressed regions.

### The Bottom Line

Energy and physical infrastructure are no longer secondary concerns for AI — they are **the primary determinant** of how fast AI capabilities advance. The companies, countries, and regions that solve the energy constraint first will lead the AI era. The roughly **$1 trillion** being invested in 2025–2026 alone represents the largest infrastructure buildout in human history, driven by a single technology: artificial intelligence.
