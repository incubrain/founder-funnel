# Foundry vision

## Purpose

A serial-attempt solo founder in Maharashtra sitting on another idea gets to a **defensible
go/no-go in under two weeks** — not six months of building the wrong thing. Foundry is where
the vague idea gets grilled into a sharp thesis, where a signal-capturing page goes live the
same day, and where a kill or a pivot is a cheap, cheerful outcome rather than a sunk year.
"Completion" here means completing the **validation cycle**, not finishing a product; a
feature that helps a founder build faster rather than decide faster is working against this.

The second beneficiary is the operator. Every founder who runs the loop and consents to
share the outcome sharpens incubrain's own grilling heuristics and diligence — which is what
makes foundry the front door to the investment arm rather than a marketing template.

None of this is true yet. As of August 2026 nobody outside Drew has run the loop end to end:
the whole founder-loop epic tree under `product-validator-ufs` is open, and the shipped
artefact is a Nuxt layer that runs two of our own websites.

## How I'd know

- **Founders reaching a recorded decision.** The count of founders who completed
  grill → page → go/no-go via foundry rises off zero and keeps rising, and a healthy share of
  those decisions are **kills and pivots**, not builds — a loop that only ever says "build" is
  not grilling. Look in the deal-flow view (`product-validator-36z`); before that exists, in
  the pages themselves and the grill transcripts.
- **The data loop is opted into, not extracted.** Of founders who finish a loop, a rising
  majority opt in to sharing decisions back. Floor: it never sits at zero while usage climbs.
- **Fundable founders actually surface.** Over the next one to three years, on the order of
  5-10% of loop-completers reach Drew or his connections as real candidates. Look at who
  incubrain ends up backing and ask whether foundry put them there.
- **The near-term leading tell.** The grill catches something on a real founder's idea that
  Drew would have missed himself. That is the first evidence the heuristics are worth more
  than the person holding them, and it is observable in a single transcript — long before any
  of the above have numbers.

## What would stop it

- **Usage without deal flow.** Pages shipped climbs into the thousands while opt-ins and
  surfaced fundable founders stay at zero — foundry has become a free Carrd. Stop developing
  the founder loop; keep the layer alive purely as the website substrate for
  incubrain-website and astronera-website, and treat the incubation-arm front door as a wrong
  bet. Usage is not the win condition and never was.
- **The grill doesn't survive leaving Drew's hands.** If the first cohort (targeted at the
  2027Q1 hackathon epic) needs Drew in the room to get value, the productised grill isn't a
  product. Park it and keep the grilling private to incubrain's own diligence.
- **The only path to the data is a silent one.** If opt-in rates prove the loop only works
  with ambient, ToS-buried telemetry on struggling founders, we stop instead of taking that
  route. The downside is uninsurable — one screenshot burns the credibility flywheel the
  whole personal vision rests on.
- **Resources never arrive.** Foundry is deliberately parked behind the money engines
  (manuscript-ocr, astronera) and is designed to wait. If by the end of 2027 those still
  haven't freed attention, stop holding a slot for the founder loop: foundry is a website
  layer, and the grilling heuristics stay an internal diligence asset.

## The user and the moment

A serial-attempt solo technical founder in Pune. Idea-rich and completion-poor — two side
projects already shipped and abandoned, so they know what sinking months into the wrong thing
costs. This is not their first time round the block.

The Tuesday they open foundry, they've got another idea and can feel the old trap coming: the
urge to open a code editor and build it all themselves. Instead they spend the day being
AI-grilled from a vague idea into a sharp thesis and a validation plan, and ship a zero-setup
page that captures whether anyone actually cares. Two weeks later they kill it and feel good
about it — decision paralysis broken by data and opinionated heuristics, feature creep broken
by guardrails, invisibility broken by a page that existed on day one.
